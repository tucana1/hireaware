# Latin America and the Caribbean

Scope: Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Venezuela, Honduras, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Guatemala, plus a short Caribbean note (Cuba, Dominican Republic). Research date: 2026-11-07. Citation prefix: LAC. Every source in section 5 was opened during this research; facts seen only in search snippets are marked "UNVERIFIED (snippet only)". Note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so some leads could not be chased further (listed in section 4).

## 1. Regional picture: what shapes the risk here

- **Two different kinds of economic pressure.** In Central America, households depend heavily on remittances: in 2024 they were 26.63% of GDP in Nicaragua, 25.74% in Honduras, 24.34% in El Salvador and 19.12% in Guatemala, compared with 3.71% in Mexico, 2.82% in Colombia and 0.22% in Brazil (World Bank, data updated 2026-10-08) [LAC-1]. Brazil and Colombia have large urban youth labour markets with high youth unemployment, 15.7% and 19.4% in 2024 (ILO modelled estimate via World Bank) [LAC-2], and young people with digital skills. That profile is exactly what scam-compound recruiters now ask for [LAC-10].
- **Youth idleness is widespread.** The share of young people not in education, employment or training (NEET) in 2024 was 30.8% in Honduras, 24.0% in Guatemala, 23.2% in Colombia, 20.5% in El Salvador, 19.3% in Brazil and 15.9% in Mexico. Venezuela's last reported figure was 37.1% in 2020 (World Bank, 2026-10-08) [LAC-3]. Vulnerable (own-account or family) employment ranges from 25.3% of jobs in Mexico to 43.1% in Colombia (2024) [LAC-4].
- **Who runs the disinformation industry differs by country.**
  - *State-run or state-contracted* operations are documented in Venezuela (the government paid citizens through the Patria platform to tweet the ministry's "hashtag of the day") [LAC-44], Nicaragua (a troll farm run mainly by staff of the state telecoms and postal regulator, TELCOR) [LAC-58], Honduras (fake accounts and Pages linked to the government's social-media staff) [LAC-50][LAC-51] and El Salvador (troll farms with contracted staff overseen by officials) [LAC-55].
  - *Commercial or campaign markets* are documented in Brazil (WhatsApp mass-messaging agencies in 2018) [LAC-7], Guatemala ("netcenters" run out of marketing firms) [LAC-63], Colombia ("bodegas" of coordinated accounts and paid influencers) [LAC-38][LAC-39], and through Predictvia, a Miami-registered firm working from Venezuela and the US that targeted Guatemala and Honduras [LAC-48].
- **Workers often did not know what the job really was.** A Salvadoran communications specialist said they did not know what the work involved when they signed a US$600-a-month contract and later called themselves a pro-Bukele troll [LAC-55]. A Venezuelan "tuitera" who ran more than 15 accounts said she did not know what a bot account was and did not realise she was amplifying propaganda [LAC-46]. Guatemalan netcenter operators, by contrast, worked from scripts for paying clients [LAC-63].
- **Spanish and Portuguese speakers are a newly targeted labour pool for Southeast Asian scam compounds.** In 2025, Cambodia was the destination in 44 of the 84 cases (52.4%) of Brazilians identified as victims of international trafficking [LAC-17]. Colombia's migration authority reported 127 trafficking rescues between July 2025 and mid-December 2025 (49% Venezuelan, 45% Colombian), and a UNODC spokesperson pointed to new markets in Latin America and Spain. Networks reportedly want native Spanish speakers because machine translation is not convincing enough [LAC-30].
- **Victims face legal risk both abroad and at home.** At least 26 Brazilians were arrested as suspects in a Laos scam raid in July 2026 [LAC-16]. A Brazilian woman recruited for "telemarketing" in Cambodia was sentenced there to 2 years 8 months on a drug charge that her family disputes [LAC-18]. At home, a Brazilian recruiter received a final sentence of 23 years 10 months [LAC-11]. Itamaraty (Brazil's foreign ministry) says victims are pressured to recruit other Brazilians [LAC-10], which can expose them to trafficking charges (inference, not a documented prosecution).
- **The war economy is a second recruitment channel.** A 2026 rights report cited by CiberCuba says Spanish-language Facebook ads offering construction, warehouse or security jobs led Cubans into Russian military contracts. It also describes a Colombian firm reportedly recruiting ex-soldiers [LAC-43]. Russia's Alabuga Start programme began recruiting women aged 18–22 in South America in 2024 and by 2025 had recruitment channels in 13 South American countries [LAC-67][LAC-66].
- **The 2026–27 election calendar drives demand for paid online campaigning.** Colombia (June 2026) and Brazil (October 2026) have voted [LAC-42][LAC-23]. Still ahead: El Salvador (28 Feb 2027) [LAC-56], Mexico (6 Jun 2027) [LAC-29], Guatemala (June 2027, presumptive) [LAC-64], Nicaragua (postponed to late 2027) [LAC-60] and an undated Venezuelan vote [LAC-49].

## 2. Country profiles

### 2.1 Brazil

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money**
  - Youth unemployment (ages 15–24): 15.7% in 2024, 17.9% in 2023 (ILO modelled, World Bank, 2026-10-08) [LAC-2].
  - NEET share: 19.3% (2024) [LAC-3]. Vulnerable employment: 26.3% (2024) [LAC-4]. Remittances are negligible at 0.22% of GDP (2024) [LAC-1].
  - **Betting debt is a major household stressor.**
    - Monthly money flows to online betting were R$18–21 billion during 2024, mostly via Pix, and about 24 million Brazilians bet (Gazeta do Povo, 2024-09-24, citing a Banco Central technical analysis) [LAC-6].
    - In August 2024, 5 million members of Bolsa Família (welfare) households sent about R$3 billion to betting firms, with a median of R$100 per person [LAC-6].
- **Migration & agencies**
  - Itamaraty says Southeast Asia has become the main destination for trafficking of Brazilians for labour exploitation.
  - Its alert of 24 Feb 2026 warns against offers with high pay, fast hiring or informal middlemen (SBT News, 2026-02-25) [LAC-10].
  - Brazilian victims of international trafficking identified under the POP-TIP protocol rose to 84 in 2025 from 63 the year before (+33.3%). Destinations: Cambodia 44 (52.4%), Israel 11, Italy 6, Belgium 6, Albania 5 (SBT News, 2026-08-01, citing Ministry of Justice data) [LAC-17].
- **Who is targeted**
  - Itamaraty says recruiters target young people with IT or computer skills through social networks [LAC-10].
  - Women were 53.6% of POP-TIP international victims in 2025. In forced-labour cases handled by the federal public defender's office (DPU), 82.1% of victims were men [LAC-17].
  - Of the 26 Brazilians arrested in Laos in July 2026, 8 were women [LAC-16].
- **Platforms & payments**
  - Recruitment runs through social networks (Itamaraty; specific apps not named) [LAC-10].
  - WhatsApp was the channel for paid mass political messaging in 2018 [LAC-7].
  - Pix carries most betting transfers [LAC-6].
  - One Brazilian was reportedly recruited via LinkedIn with a tech job that turned out to be a Russian military contract (CiberCuba citing a FIDH/Truth Hounds report) [LAC-43].
  - Kwai and TikTok penetration figures: UNVERIFIED (not retrieved).
- **Politics & elections 2026–27**
  - In the 25 Oct 2026 runoff, Lula won 51.75% to Flávio Bolsonaro's 48.25% (preliminary TSE figures; Keystone-SDA, 2026-10-26) [LAC-23].
  - Next national vote: 2030.
- **Disinfo industry model:** mainly commercial. Businesses bought WhatsApp mass-messaging packages from marketing agencies in 2018 [LAC-7].
- **Legal risks to victims**
  - Arrest abroad as scam suspects (Laos, July 2026) [LAC-16].
  - Prosecution abroad on other charges (Cambodia, 2025) [LAC-18].
  - Victims are pressured to recruit other Brazilians [LAC-10], which creates exposure under Brazil's trafficking law (inference).
- **Consular reach**
  - Brazil has embassies in Bangkok and Yangon, which followed the 2024–25 KK Park case (CNN Brasil, 2024-12-20) [LAC-8], and in Phnom Penh [LAC-18]. The Bangkok embassy also covers Laos (Jornal de Brasília, 2026-07-23) [LAC-16].
  - A rescued Brazilian said the government only paid his ticket home, while NGO The Exodus Road did the extraction [LAC-9].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **2018 WhatsApp mass messaging (Folha de S.Paulo investigation, 18 Oct 2018, as summarised by Olhar Digital the same day)** [LAC-7]
  - Businesses backing Jair Bolsonaro reportedly bought bulk WhatsApp messaging against the PT (Workers' Party), at up to R$12 million per contract and R$0.08–0.40 per message.
  - Agencies named: Quickmobile, Yacows, Croc Services and SMS Market.
  - Phone numbers came from supporters and from third-party databases that may have included leaked data, which let messages be targeted by region, age and class.
  - The legal issue was undeclared corporate campaign donations ("caixa 2").
  - The agencies and the campaign denied wrongdoing.
  - How the agencies' own operators were hired and paid was not described. UNVERIFIED: later reporting that SIM cards were registered with third parties' tax IDs (CPFs) was not opened.

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- **Final conviction of a recruiter** (MPF, 2026-05-26) [LAC-11]
  - A Brazilian man received a final sentence of 23 years 10 months for trafficking 12 Brazilians to KK Park (Myanmar) via Bangkok.
  - The lure was telemarketing work in Thailand at US$1,500 a month, with flights, food and lodging paid. He used social-media posts showing a fake luxury lifestyle.
  - He reportedly received US$500 per person from a Chinese criminal group.
  - Victims worked shifts of more than 14 hours and built up debts and fines.
- **KK Park, 2024–25**
  - Two men (26 and 31) flew to Bangkok in October and November 2024 for supposed casino and call-centre jobs and were held at KK Park (CNN Brasil, 2024-12-20) [LAC-8].
  - They escaped in February 2025 with help from The Exodus Road. The NGO estimated at least 8 Brazilians were still held (Jornal de Brasília/Folhapress, 2025-02-20) [LAC-9].
- **Itamaraty alert, 24 Feb 2026** [LAC-10]
  - Embassies reported a growing pattern of offers aimed specifically at Brazilians: attractive pay, paid airfare, fake call-centre or "technology company" jobs.
  - Victims are then forced into gambling, crypto and romance scams and pressured to recruit other Brazilians.
- **Recruitment case in Cambodia** (Itatiaia, 2025-11-12) [LAC-18]
  - A 35-year-old architect from Belo Horizonte found a telemarketing job in Phnom Penh online at about US$2,000 a month, with travel, lodging and meals paid.
  - When she tried to leave she was charged a US$4,000 "termination fee". Her family paid R$27,000 in total.
  - She was then detained after police said they found drug capsules, and sentenced to 2 years 8 months.
- **Interpol Operation Global Chain, 8–12 June 2026**
  - Brazil's Federal Police (PF) dismantled a network sending victims to Cambodia's scam operations and identified 406 victims: 83 Brazilian and 323 foreign (Primicias citing Interpol, 2026-07-06) [LAC-14].
  - The MPF (federal prosecutors) charged three Brazilians and one Chinese national. Two of the Brazilians are held in China awaiting extradition. Victims were forced to scam other Brazilians (Gazeta do Povo, 2026-07-21) [LAC-13].
  - Global totals: 59 countries, 1,024 arrests, 2,070 victims or potential victims (Frontex, 2026-07-06) [LAC-15].
- **PF action, 14 July 2026:** search in São José dos Campos (SP). The case began from an Itamaraty report on repatriated Brazilians from the Cambodia–Vietnam border. One suspect in Cambodia faces preventive arrest and an Interpol Red Notice (PF, 2026-07-14) [LAC-12].
- **Laos, July 2026** (Jornal de Brasília/Folha, 2026-07-23) [LAC-16]
  - 26 Brazilians were among 122 people arrested in Vientiane.
  - Laos reported 4,482 detentions of 25 nationalities in the first half of 2026.
  - Itamaraty would not say whether the Brazilians are victims. The Exodus Road Brasil treats them as trafficking cases.
- **Lure inflation:** some recruiters offered up to US$10,000 a month, and victims reported working 15–18 hours a day (SBT News, 2026-08-01) [LAC-17].

### 2.2 Mexico

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money:**
  - Youth unemployment: 5.8% (2024) [LAC-2]. NEET share: 15.9% (2024) [LAC-3]. Vulnerable employment: 25.3% (2024) [LAC-4].
  - Remittances: 3.71% of GDP (2024) [LAC-1].
  - Household debt figures: UNVERIFIED (not retrieved).
- **Migration & agencies:**
  - The main labour corridor is to the US and Canada. The Centro de los Derechos del Migrante runs Contratados.org, where workers can search and review employers, recruiters and agencies (toll-free 800-590-1773 from Mexico) [LAC-28].
  - Reported scale of fake H-2 visa recruiter fraud: UNVERIFIED (not retrieved).
- **Who is targeted:** no Mexico-specific data on scam-compound recruitment was found (see section 4).
- **Platforms & payments:**
  - Twitter/X bot networks were documented in 2012–15 [LAC-24].
  - Usage shares for WhatsApp, Facebook and TikTok, and for payment rails (SPEI, CoDi/DiMo, cash at OXXO): UNVERIFIED (not retrieved).
- **Politics & elections 2026–27:** federal legislative elections on 6 June 2027 for all 500 seats in the Chamber of Deputies, alongside state elections (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-07) [LAC-29]. Whether a second judicial election falls in 2027 or 2028: UNVERIFIED.
- **Disinfo industry model:** mixed and murky.
  - Researchers counted more than 75,000 automated "Peñabot" accounts, but no firm link to the government was established [LAC-24].
- **Legal risks to victims:** no Mexico-specific cases found. UNVERIFIED.
- **Consular reach:**
  - The SRE (foreign ministry) runs CIAM, a 24/7 information and assistance line for Mexicans (from the US: 520-623-7874) [LAC-25].
  - Mexican embassy coverage in Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar: UNVERIFIED.

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **"Peñabots"** (Vice/Motherboard, 2015-08-24) [LAC-24]
  - Researcher Erin Gallagher estimated more than 75,000 automated Twitter accounts used to drown out protest hashtags and attack critics.
  - The accounts first appeared during Enrique Peña Nieto's 2012 campaign and pushed about 2–3 fake trends a day.
  - Operators were not identified. No evidence of how operators were paid or recruited.
- Commercial troll and bot markets after 2018: not documented in this pass (UNVERIFIED).

- Alabuga Start: FDD (Jan 2026) reportedly identified local recruiters in Mexico. UNVERIFIED (snippet only; fdd.org blocked access).

### 2.3 Colombia

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money:**
  - Youth unemployment: 19.4% (2024), the highest of the eight countries [LAC-2]. NEET share: 23.2% [LAC-3]. Vulnerable employment: 43.1% [LAC-4].
  - Remittances: 2.82% of GDP (2024) [LAC-1].
- **Migration & agencies:**
  - Only specially authorised "transnational employment agencies" may place Colombians abroad. These are family compensation funds (cajas de compensación), job banks, the SENA public employment agency and other public agencies. They must not charge workers for a job opportunity or for passports, visas or medical exams, and must not promote false job offers abroad. Their registry is public (Resolución 1481 de 2014, Ministerio del Trabajo, Arts. 2, 6, 10, 11) [LAC-37].
  - Colombia is a major host of Venezuelan migrants (per-country figures not retrieved). Venezuelan refugees and migrants in Latin America and the Caribbean number 6,978,009 (R4V, August 2026) [LAC-5].
- **Who is targeted:**
  - The ombudsman's office (Defensoría del Pueblo) recorded 109 trafficking cases from January to June 2025 (203 in all of 2024). 105 victims were women, and 89 cases were sexual exploitation (Blu Radio, 2025-07-30) [LAC-34].
  - The 20 Colombians rescued in Cambodia in November 2025 were mostly from Bogotá [LAC-31].
- **Platforms & payments:**
  - LibertApp, the migration authority's free Android/iOS reporting app, works without internet and has a geolocated panic button. It has more than 12,000 downloads (Vanguardia, 2026-07-30) [LAC-33].
  - In 2026, coordinated "bodega" networks operated on Facebook and X [LAC-38].
  - Payment rails (Nequi, Daviplata, Bre-B): UNVERIFIED (not retrieved).
- **Politics & elections 2026–27:**
  - Abelardo de la Espriella won the 21 June 2026 runoff with 12,960,166 votes to Iván Cepeda's 12,708,312, confirmed by the electoral council (CNE). Term: 2026–2030 (Blu Radio, 2026-06-24) [LAC-42].
- **Disinfo industry model:** campaign and commercial "bodegas" plus paid influencers. Allegations also concern influencers paid from state funds [LAC-38][LAC-39][LAC-40].
- **Legal risks to victims:** no documented prosecutions of Colombian scam-compound victims found. GAP.
- **Consular reach:**
  - The Cambodia rescue was coordinated by the migration authority's judicial police with Cambodian authorities after a LibertApp alert [LAC-31].
  - Colombian embassy coverage in Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar: UNVERIFIED.

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **2026 presidential race** (La Silla Vacía, 2026-06-04, republished by Chequeado 2026-06-10) [LAC-38]
  - 2,300 accounts in 22 anomalous communities posted 11,715 comments boosting the three leading candidates between February and April 2026, about 12% of all comments on their posts.
  - Pro-De la Espriella communities had 1,730 accounts. His coordinated accounts grew from 120 to 1,894. A pro-Valencia community had 202 accounts and a pro-Cepeda community 164.
  - The study does not identify who runs or pays the accounts.
- **Paid influencers** (El Colombiano, 2025-07-11) [LAC-39]
  - Former Prosperidad Social director Gustavo Bolívar said a single influencer can charge 10–15 million pesos a month for campaign work, and proposed a #PPP ("publicidad política pagada", paid political advertising) disclosure tag.
  - A councilman showed a 71.4 million-peso invoice for mentions on an online programme that he said the agency paid.
  - Bolívar also said campaigns tell paid accounts the "campaign of the day" and how much they will be paid, without giving amounts (Vanguardia, 2025-07-14) [LAC-40].
- **2023 Medellín local race** (El Colombiano, 2023-10-16) [LAC-41]
  - 8,524 mentions from 2,908 accounts, attributed to bodegas in Bogotá, Antioquia and Valle.
  - One campaign spent about 213 million pesos on 428 Meta ads while reporting 0 pesos of social-media spending to the official campaign-finance register (Cuentas Claras).

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- **Cambodia rescue, November 2025** (Vanguardia and Publimetro, 2025-11-12) [LAC-31][LAC-32]
  - 20 Colombians were rescued after accepting offers to work on supposedly "legal digital platforms" at up to US$1,000 a month.
  - Passports and phones were taken on arrival. One victim hid a phone. His wife sent his live location through LibertApp, and the migration authority (Migración Colombia) coordinated with Cambodian police.
- **2025 totals and scale** (El Imparcial with EFE interviews, 2025-12-19) [LAC-30]
  - Migración Colombia reported 127 rescues from July 2025 onward: 49% Venezuelan, 45% Colombian, 6% other. The article puts this in the scam-centre context, but the figure appears to cover all trafficking rescues.
  - Recruiters were Latin Americans. A UNODC spokesperson pointed to new markets in Latin America and Spain.
- **LibertApp results, July 2026** (Vanguardia, 2026-07-30) [LAC-33]
  - 68 victims rescued via the app: 51 Colombian, 15 Venezuelan, 1 Ecuadorian, 1 Panamanian; 3 minors.
  - Exploitation types: 56 sexual exploitation, 10 forced labour.
  - About 2,500 citizen reports, more than 200 panic-button activations and 350 cases formalised for investigation.
- **Russian army**
  - A FIDH/Truth Hounds report, as cited by CiberCuba (2026-04-30), says a Colombian firm reportedly led by two retired army colonels offered ex-soldiers "security" jobs at €2,200–2,500 a month plus an initial payment of about €17,000, which ended in transfers to Russian ranks.
  - Ukrainian intelligence confirmed Colombian fighters in Russian ranks in October 2025 [LAC-43].
  - PARTLY VERIFIED: the opened source is secondary; the FIDH report itself was not opened.

### 2.4 Venezuela

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money:**
  - Youth unemployment: 10.2% (2024, modelled) [LAC-2]. NEET share: 37.1% (2020, latest available) [LAC-3]. Vulnerable employment: 37.2% [LAC-4].
  - Remittances: 3.16% of GDP (2024) [LAC-1].
- **Migration & agencies:**
  - There are 6,978,009 Venezuelan refugees and migrants in Latin America and the Caribbean (R4V, last updated August 2026). R4V notes the real total is likely higher because many lack regular status [LAC-5].
  - Venezuelans made up 49% of Migración Colombia's 127 trafficking rescues in the second half of 2025 [LAC-30] and 15 of the 68 LibertApp rescues [LAC-33].
- **Who is targeted:** in the documented Patria tweeting scheme, payments went to ordinary card-holders. One example was a 39-year-old single mother without a stable job, running accounts for relatives [LAC-46].
- **Platforms & payments:**
  - Twitter accounts were linked to the VeMonedero wallet of the "Carnet de la Patria" (Homeland Card) system [LAC-44].
  - Tutorials circulated in Facebook, WhatsApp and Telegram groups [LAC-47].
  - Pago Móvil and other payment rails: UNVERIFIED (not retrieved).
- **Politics & elections 2026–27:**
  - Delcy Rodríguez has been acting president since January 2026, after a US military operation removed Nicolás Maduro.
  - As of 29 Oct 2026 no election date had been set. Talks with the opposition since August 2026 cover renewing the Supreme Court and electoral council and holding regional elections first (MercoPress, 2026-10-29) [LAC-49].
- **Disinfo industry model:**
  - State-run for the domestic audience (communications ministry hashtags plus Patria payments) [LAC-44].
  - Venezuelan territory also hosted a commercial operator, Predictvia, which ran campaigns aimed at Guatemala and Honduras [LAC-48].
- **Legal risks to victims:** irregular status abroad [LAC-5]. Specific prosecutions: none found.
- **Consular reach:** UNVERIFIED (not researched).

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **Patria "bonos" for tweeting** (DFRLab, 2019-02-04) [LAC-44]
  - Users linked their Twitter accounts to the VeMonedero wallet and tweeted the daily hashtag set by the communications ministry (MIPPCI).
  - One award documented was 350,000 bolívars: about US$5 at the official rate, or US$0.50 on the black market, close to a third of the minimum monthly wage.
  - The Patria account said it rewarded about 5,000 users in one week.
  - A sign-up site asked for a Twitter handle, a Venezuelan phone number and an SMS code.
- **Platform enforcement**
  - Twitter suspended 277 accounts on 2 Dec 2021. Many had authorised "Twitter Patria" apps that let the government monitor activity and pay per tweet.
  - Tutorials set targets of at least 400 tweets a day (Caracas Chronicles/ProBox, 2021-12-17) [LAC-47].
  - Cazadores de Fake News counted at least 2,000 suspended accounts in 2021 (estimate about 3,000) and at least 570 between 26 Jan and 9 Feb 2022 (2022-02-09) [LAC-45].
- **Worker awareness** (Cazadores de Fake News, 2023-03-26) [LAC-46]
  - A 39-year-old single mother in Cojedes ran more than 15 accounts belonging to family and friends and earned up to US$80 a month.
  - She said she did not know what a bot was and believed the payments were meant to count people who stayed in Venezuela. The researchers concluded she did not realise she was amplifying propaganda.
- **Commercial spill-over** (Reuters via ThePrint, 2023-05-04) [LAC-48]
  - Meta banned Predictvia, which operated from Venezuela and the US, and removed 24 Facebook accounts, 54 Pages and 4 Instagram accounts targeting Guatemala and Honduras.
  - Linked Twitter networks reportedly numbered more than 1,300 accounts (Honduras) and 2,000 (Guatemala).

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- Venezuelans appear in Colombian rescue statistics [LAC-30][LAC-33].

## 3. Synthesis for job seekers in Latin America and the Caribbean

### 3.1 What to know
- **A "customer service", "telemarketing" or "tech company" job in Thailand, Cambodia, Laos or Myanmar, paid in dollars with a free flight, is the most common documented trap for Brazilians and Colombians right now.** Brazil's foreign ministry issued a specific warning in February 2026 [LAC-10]. Courts and police describe offers of US$1,000–2,000 a month, and some recruiters claim up to US$10,000 [LAC-11][LAC-18][LAC-31][LAC-17].
- **Your Spanish or Portuguese is what the scam bosses are buying.** They want native speakers to scam people in your own language, sometimes your own compatriots [LAC-30][LAC-13]. Treat "bilingual" or "Spanish-speaking customer service in Asia" as a red flag.
- **The recruiter may be someone like you.** Recruiters are often a friend of a friend or someone posting a luxury lifestyle online. One Brazilian recruiter was paid US$500 per person delivered [LAC-11][LAC-17]. Once inside, people are pushed to recruit others, which can turn a victim into a suspect [LAC-10].
- **Leaving costs money, and you can be arrested as a scammer.** Families have paid "termination fees" or ransoms (R$27,000 in one case). Brazilians have been jailed in Cambodia and arrested in Laos raids [LAC-18][LAC-16].
- **Check before you go.**
  - Colombia: only authorised transnational agencies may place you abroad, and it is illegal for them to charge you any fee (Res. 1481/2014) [LAC-37]. Call 01 8000 52 20 20 to ask whether an offer looks suspicious [LAC-35][LAC-36].
  - Mexico: look up the recruiter on Contratados.org [LAC-28].
  - Brazil: there is no licence check, so refuse fast hiring and informal middlemen [LAC-10].
- **"Online campaign" side-jobs can be propaganda work.** Documented examples:
  - tweeting the "hashtag of the day" for bonuses in Venezuela [LAC-44];
  - running fake accounts for US$600 a month under a gag contract in El Salvador [LAC-55];
  - "netcenter" work in Guatemala [LAC-63];
  - paid "bodega" accounts and influencers in Colombia [LAC-38][LAC-39].
  
  Ask who the client is. Refuse to run many accounts or sign a non-disclosure agreement that threatens prison.
- **War-economy lures:** "security guard", "construction" or "warehouse" jobs in Russia, or a LinkedIn "tech job", have ended in military contracts [LAC-43]. "Hotel, driving or logistics" work-study offers for women aged 18–22 (Alabuga Start) led to drone-factory work [LAC-67][LAC-66].
- **Save these numbers before you travel:**
  - Brazil: Disque 100 (WhatsApp (61) 99611-0100) [LAC-19]; Itamaraty emergency line +55 61 98260-0610 [LAC-22].
  - Mexico: anti-trafficking line 800 5533 000 [LAC-25]; 089 anonymous reporting [LAC-27]; 088 cybercrime [LAC-26].
  - Colombia: 01 8000 52 20 20 or +57 1 600 10 35 [LAC-36]; the LibertApp panic button [LAC-33].
