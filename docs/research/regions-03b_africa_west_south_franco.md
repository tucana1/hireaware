# Sub-Saharan Africa — Part B: West, Southern & Francophone Africa

_Scope: Nigeria, Ghana, South Africa, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire, Cameroon, Mali & Burkina Faso, Central African Republic (CAR), plus the Russia-linked influence networks that recruited local people (CAR, Mali, Madagascar, Sudan, Mozambique) and "African Initiative". Research date: 2026-11-07. Kenya/Uganda/Ethiopia are in Part A; Tunisia's UReputation and Israel's Archimedes Group (which also targeted West and Central African elections) are in the MENA section. Aggregate figures (OHCHR/UNODC scale, the Feb–Mar 2025 Myawaddy crackdown, Oct–Nov 2025 KK Park raids, Alabuga Start overall, Russian-army recruitment overall) are in the cross-cutting section._

_Method note: every fact line cites a page that was opened for this research. Where the original publisher blocked access (BBC, CNN, AP, DW, Guardian, Africa Center, EEAS), a faithful reproduction or a different reputable outlet was opened instead and is the one cited. Three facts rely on Wikipedia summaries and are marked "secondary". The shared web-search budget for this research run ran out partway through, so a few late checks were done only by opening known URLs; remaining gaps are listed in section 4._

## 1. Regional picture: what shapes the risk here

- **Two different "disinformation labour markets".** In the Anglophone markets (Nigeria, Ghana, South Africa) the documented model is commercial or partisan: parties pay influencers, a foreign-funded NGO hires young graduates as posters, or a PR firm is hired by a business family. A Nigerian politician told the BBC his party paid one influencer up to ₦20 million ($45,000) for "delivering a result" (Sahara Reporters reproducing BBC, 18 Jan 2023) [AFW-1]; EBLA employed 16 Ghanaians, mostly in their 20s, in a walled compound near Accra (Graphic Online reproducing CNN, 13 Mar 2020) [AFW-2]; Bell Pottinger was expelled from the UK PR trade body for at least five years over its Gupta-funded campaign (Press Gazette, 5 Sep 2017) [AFW-7]. In the Sahel and Central Africa, the dominant model is foreign-state influence (Russian, and in one documented case French military) that recruits local page administrators, journalists and "correspondents", increasingly fused with junta-run state propaganda [AFW-5][AFW-55][AFW-56].
- **Russia's networks deliberately use real local people; France's used fake ones.** Meta found Russia-linked networks in CAR relied on local nationals from CAR and South Africa (paying for ads mainly in rand and dollars), whereas the French military-linked network posed as locals with fake accounts: ~$38,000 in ad spend for the Russian CAR network (Meta, 15 Dec 2020) [AFW-5]. In 2019, Meta said Prigozhin-linked operators mixed fake accounts with authentic accounts of local nationals in Madagascar and Mozambique: 35 Facebook accounts, 53 Pages, ~475,000 followers (Meta, 30 Oct 2019) [AFW-4]. Meta's security lead said the local actors appeared to know who was behind the operations: (VICE, 30 Oct 2019) [AFW-6]. In Ghana, by contrast, Meta said EBLA's operators included people who joined "wittingly and unwittingly": (Meta, 12 Mar 2020) [AFW-3].
- **Headline unemployment hides the real problem in West Africa.** ILO-modelled youth unemployment is low in Francophone/West Africa (Senegal 3.6%, Côte d'Ivoire 4.0%, Nigeria 5.3%, Ghana 5.8%, Cameroon 6.5% in 2025) because most young people work informally; vulnerable (own-account or unpaid family) employment is 60–69% of all jobs (World Bank/ILO modelled, 2025; updated 8 Oct 2026) [AFW-30][AFW-31][AFW-32]. South Africa is the opposite case: open youth unemployment of 47.4% for ages 15–34 in Q2 2026 (SAnews/Stats SA, 11 Aug 2026) [AFW-19]. Practical effect: West African lures sell "a real salary in dollars"; South African lures sell "any job at all".
- **Remittance dependence is highest in Senegal and Nigeria.** Personal remittances equal 10.6% of GDP in Senegal (2023) and 7.8% in Nigeria (2025), versus 2–4% in Ghana, Côte d'Ivoire, Mali and Burkina Faso and 0.24% in South Africa (World Bank WDI API, accessed 7 Nov 2026) [AFW-28][AFW-29]. Families that depend on a migrant's income are a recruitment channel and a pressure point.
- **Scam compounds recruit from this sub-region, and the industry has also arrived here.** Nigerians and South Africans were recruited through Facebook/social-media ads for "customer care", "marketing", "teaching" or "hospitality" jobs in Thailand and trafficked into Myanmar compounds [AFW-9][AFW-10][AFW-36]. In December 2024 Nigeria's EFCC arrested 792 people, including 148 Chinese and 40 Filipino nationals, at a Lagos building run as a crypto-romance call centre, where Nigerians made first contact with foreign victims (Africanews, 17 Dec 2024) [AFW-34]. NAPTIP describes a new pattern of targeting healthy, IT-skilled youths (Nigeria Info, 11 Feb 2026) [AFW-35].
- **Russia's war economy is now a major deceptive-recruitment destination for this sub-region.** Ghana says 272 citizens were lured into the war since 2022 and about 55 killed (Africanews, 27 Feb 2026) [AFW-40]; Cameroon confirmed 16 deaths in April 2026 (Africanews, 7 Apr 2026) [AFW-51]; 17 South African men were lured with "security/bodyguard training" (SAnews, 6 Nov 2025; Al Jazeera, 5 Mar 2026) [AFW-14][AFW-18]; Togolese and Senegalese students were recruited through scholarship offers [AFW-38][AFW-61]. South African influencers promoted Alabuga Start to young women (ADF Magazine, 7 Oct 2025) [AFW-13].
- **Election calendars drive demand for paid posters.** Nigeria votes for president on 16 January 2027 (campaigns opened 19 August 2026) (NAN, 27 Feb 2026) [AFW-33]; South Africa held local elections on 4 November 2026 (SAnews, 7 Aug 2026) [AFW-20]; Côte d'Ivoire, Cameroon and CAR held contested presidential votes in Oct–Dec 2025 [AFW-46][AFW-49][AFW-59]; Mali and Burkina Faso have no elections in sight and have dissolved political parties [AFW-53][AFW-54].
- **Victims face legal exposure.** Nigerians who escaped a Myanmar compound were charged in Thailand with illegal immigration and pleaded guilty before deportation (Vanguard, 7 Mar 2026) [AFW-36]; South Africans who fought for Russia are being investigated under the Foreign Military Assistance Act (Al Jazeera, 5 Mar 2026) [AFW-18]; workers in Ghana's EBLA troll farm were held at gunpoint and interrogated in a security raid (Graphic/CNN, 13 Mar 2020) [AFW-2].

## 2. Country profiles

### 2.1 Nigeria

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money:**
  - ILO-modelled youth (15–24) unemployment is low: 5.34% (World Bank/ILO, 2025 estimate; updated 8 Oct 2026) [AFW-30]
  - Vulnerable employment is high: 66.5% of total employment (World Bank/ILO, 2025) [AFW-32]
  - Remittances are a large share of the economy: 7.8% of GDP (World Bank, 2025) [AFW-28]
  - Household debt and cost-of-living data were not verified in this pass (see section 4).
- **Migration & agencies:**
  - The Federal Ministry of Labour and Employment issues separate recruiter licences for domestic and international placement. It says organisations without a Ministry licence that charge fees are most likely fraudulent: 19 licence requirements, including NSITF registration and a bank guarantee (FMLE, page accessed 7 Nov 2026) [AFW-39]
  - The emigration wave known as "japa" is widely discussed, but no emigration figure was verified in this pass.
- **Who is targeted:**
  - NAPTIP says traffickers target young Nigerians with computer/IT skills who are in good health. Some victims were put in language schools so they could work as translators or customer-care agents defrauding people in the US, UK, Canada and Ethiopia: 23 youths rescued (Nigeria Info, 11 Feb 2026) [AFW-35]
  - Reporting on Alabuga Start says dozens of Nigerian secondary-school leavers, mostly women aged 18–22, were enrolled at Alabuga Polytechnic and ended up assembling drones (Vanguard via AllAfrica, 1 Jun 2025) [AFW-37]
  - Nigeria's Ministry of Foreign Affairs denied facilitating or endorsing the scheme: (Vanguard via AllAfrica, 1 Jun 2025) [AFW-37]
- **Platforms & payments:**
  - Internet use and social media accounts: 109 million internet users (45.5%); 47.8 million social-media identities (DataReportal, Digital 2026: Nigeria, 8 Nov 2025) [AFW-23]
  - TikTok has the widest ad reach among adults, followed by Facebook and YouTube: TikTok 47.8 million adults (38.2% of 18+), Facebook 38.0 million, YouTube 30.5 million, X 6.95 million (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [AFW-23]
  - Scam recruiters advertised on Facebook, then moved applicants into Telegram groups for "structured interviews": (Vanguard, 7 Mar 2026) [AFW-36]
  - Party payments to influencers were mostly in cash, to avoid a paper trail: (Sahara Reporters/BBC, 18 Jan 2023) [AFW-1]
  - Fintech and mobile-money wallets (OPay, PalmPay, Moniepoint) are UNVERIFIED: no source was opened in this pass.
- **Politics & elections 2026–27:**
  - INEC moved the 2027 general elections earlier after the Electoral Act 2026 replaced the 2022 Act. Presidential and National Assembly polls are on 16 Jan 2027, and governorship and state assembly polls on 6 Feb 2027 (both previously Feb/Mar 2027). Campaigns opened 19 Aug 2026 and 9 Sep 2026 (NAN, 27 Feb 2026) [AFW-33]
  - Any later change to this timetable was not checked; see section 4.
- **Disinfo industry model:**
  - Mainly commercial/partisan. Parties pay influencers with cash, gifts, government contracts and political appointments, scaled to follower counts, and some influencers subcontract "micro-influencers": up to ₦20m per influencer (Sahara Reporters/BBC, 18 Jan 2023) [AFW-1]
  - Nigeria has also been used as offshore labour for foreign operations: EBLA filled at least 8 positions in Lagos (Graphic/CNN, 13 Mar 2020) [AFW-2]
- **Legal risks to victims:**
  - Nigerians who escaped a Myanmar compound on 1 Dec 2025 were taken into Thai military custody, charged with illegal immigration, pleaded guilty, and were held at Bangkok's immigration detention centre before deportation in Jan 2026 (Vanguard, 7 Mar 2026) [AFW-36]
  - At home, people working on the "front line" of a foreign-run scam centre can themselves be arrested: 792 arrested in one EFCC raid (Africanews, 17 Dec 2024) [AFW-34]
- **Consular reach:**
  - Returns from Thailand were coordinated by NAPTIP, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Nigerian Embassy in Bangkok and the NGO EDEN: more than 32 Nigerians returned in batches (Vanguard, 7 Mar 2026) [AFW-36]
  - Whether Nigeria has a resident mission in Myanmar is UNVERIFIED.

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **2023 election influencers (BBC Global Disinformation Team).**
  - Whistle-blowers from two unnamed parties described secretly paying influencers to spread false stories about rivals: up to ₦20m ($45,000; £37,000) for one influencer (Sahara Reporters reproducing BBC, 18 Jan 2023) [AFW-1]
  - Rewards included cash, gifts, government contracts and appointments such as board seats or "special assistant" posts. Pay was scaled to follower counts and mostly made in cash: (Sahara Reporters/BBC, 18 Jan 2023) [AFW-1]
  - An influencer with about 150,000 Facebook followers confirmed being paid to post completely false stories about opponents, so the workers knew: (Sahara Reporters/BBC, 18 Jan 2023) [AFW-1]
- **EBLA's Lagos office (2019–2020).**
  - The Russia-linked NGO EBLA recruited Nigerians alongside Ghanaians and advertised roles such as a "social media management" project manager: at least 8 positions filled in Nigeria (Graphic/CNN, 13 Mar 2020) [AFW-2]
  - Meta attributed the combined Ghana–Nigeria network to EBLA and to people linked to the Internet Research Agency, saying it was run by locals working for individuals in Russia: 49 Facebook accounts, 69 Pages, 85 Instagram accounts (Meta, 12 Mar 2020) [AFW-3]

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- **NAPTIP rescue (Feb 2026).** Victims were lured with scholarships and lucrative jobs, then forced into romance, crypto and investment fraud in Myanmar, Laos and Cambodia: 23 rescued and repatriated via Thailand (Nigeria Info, 11 Feb 2026) [AFW-35]
- **Survivor account (Vanguard, Mar 2026).** The pipeline had several steps:
  - A Facebook ad for "customer care agents" in Thailand offered pay, accommodation and meals, with "no upfront payment": up to $1,500/month (Vanguard, 7 Mar 2026) [AFW-36]
  - Applicants were moved to Telegram interviews and asked for passport pages, a birth certificate, police clearance, a typing-test video and bank statements. Recruiters paid "show money" into applicants' accounts to pass visa and airport checks.
  - Recruits were coached to say they were tourists heading to Mae Sot, then trucked across the border at night.
  - In the compound they ran romance scams for up to 18 hours a day. Punishments included beatings and electric shocks, and one Nigerian died: 5 survivors interviewed; journey Sep 2025, escape 1 Dec 2025, return Jan 2026 (Vanguard, 7 Mar 2026) [AFW-36]
- **Scam centre at home (Dec 2024).**
  - In a raid on the seven-storey Big Leaf Building in Lagos on 10 Dec 2024, the EFCC arrested 792 people, including 148 Chinese and 40 Filipinos. Nigerians made first contact with victims in the Americas and Europe on WhatsApp and Instagram (Africanews, 17 Dec 2024) [AFW-34]
  - The EFCC's own release page did not load, so its recruitment and training details are UNVERIFIED.
- **Alabuga Start.**
  - Allegations of Nigerian school-leavers recruited to Alabuga Polytechnic: (Vanguard via AllAfrica, 1 Jun 2025) [AFW-37]
  - The Russian embassy called the reports false (13 May 2025 statement) [AFW-37]
  - The claim that a Federal Ministry of Education document advertised Alabuga admissions in 2023 appears in search summaries only: UNVERIFIED (snippet only).
- **Russian army.** No verified Nigeria-specific count was found in this pass (see section 4).

### 2.2 Ghana

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money:**
  - ILO-modelled youth unemployment: 5.83% (World Bank/ILO, 2025) [AFW-30]
  - Vulnerable employment: 60.0% (World Bank/ILO, 2025) [AFW-32]
  - Remittances: 2.1% of GDP (World Bank, 2025) [AFW-28]
- **Migration & agencies:**
  - The Ministry of Employment and Labour Relations says its list of licensed private employment agencies allowed to recruit Ghanaians for work abroad is published and updated on melr.gov.gh, and that agency status can be checked with the Labour Department: (Ghana News Agency, 1 Feb 2024) [AFW-41]
  - The same notice debunked a viral recruitment video about Canadian oil-field jobs [AFW-41]
- **Who is targeted:**
  - EBLA hired Ghanaians mostly in their 20s; some lived rent-free in a nearby flat: 16 workers (Graphic/CNN, 13 Mar 2020) [AFW-2]
  - Ghanaians sent to the Russia–Ukraine war were mostly promised well-paid civilian jobs: 272 lured since 2022 (Africanews, 27 Feb 2026) [AFW-40]
- **Platforms & payments:**
  - Internet and social media: 26.3 million internet users (74.6%); 8.59 million social-media identities (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [AFW-24]
  - Ad reach: YouTube 8.59 million; Facebook 8.40 million (23.8%); LinkedIn 3.4 million; X 1.17 million (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [AFW-24]
  - Mobile money (MTN MoMo) is UNVERIFIED: no source was opened.
- **Politics & elections 2026–27:** No national election was identified for 2026–27. The next general election (expected December 2028) is UNVERIFIED.
- **Disinfo industry model:** Commercial outsourcing for a foreign client. EBLA ran as a local NGO paying young Ghanaians to post for a Russian-linked operation; Ghanaian security sources told CNN all its funding came from Russia (Graphic/CNN, 13 Mar 2020) [AFW-2]
- **Legal risks to victims:** In the 6 Feb 2020 raid on EBLA, workers say they were made to lie on the ground at gunpoint, interrogated, and had their phones confiscated. Ghana's Cyber Security Unit suspected "organized radicalism" with foreign links (Graphic/CNN, 13 Mar 2020) [AFW-2]
- **Consular reach:** The foreign minister travelled to Kyiv over two Ghanaians held as prisoners of war (Africanews, 27 Feb 2026) [AFW-40]

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment: EBLA (2019–2020)
- **Set-up.**
  - Eliminating Barriers for the Liberation of Africa (EBLA) rented a walled compound in a residential area near Accra and employed Ghanaians, mostly in their 20s: 16 workers (Graphic/CNN, 13 Mar 2020) [AFW-2]
  - It was run by Seth Wiredu, a Ghanaian living in Russia who called himself "Mr. Amara" and claimed to be South African. He denied links to the IRA [AFW-2]
- **What workers did and how they were paid.**
  - Workers were given US topics (LGBT issues, police brutality) and US articles, and told to post in the late afternoon or night Ghana time to reach US audiences [AFW-2]
  - One worker said bonuses and higher pay came if accounts grew. No salary figures were published (see section 4).
- **Whether they knew.**
  - One worker told CNN she "had no idea" she would be working as a Russian troll [AFW-2]
  - Meta described the operators as locals acting "wittingly and unwittingly" on behalf of people in Russia [AFW-3]
  - After the raid, Wiredu told workers to create new accounts and send him the passwords [AFW-2]
- **Scale of the takedown.**
  - Meta removed 49 Facebook accounts, 69 Pages and 85 Instagram accounts. About 13,500 accounts followed the Pages and about 265,000 followed the Instagram accounts, ~65% of them in the US. Ad spend was about $379 (Meta, 12 Mar 2020) [AFW-3]
  - Twitter's removal of 71 accounts is UNVERIFIED (snippet only).

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- **Russian army.**
  - The foreign minister said 272 Ghanaians had been lured into the war since 2022 by "criminal trafficking networks": about 55 killed, 2 captured (Africanews, 27 Feb 2026) [AFW-40]
  - Most were promised well-paid civilian jobs, then made to sign army contracts and sent to fight with little training [AFW-40]
  - Ghana pledged to track and dismantle illegal dark-web recruitment schemes [AFW-40]
- **Scam compounds.** No Ghana-specific rescue figures were found in this pass (see section 4).

### 2.3 South Africa

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money:**
  - Official unemployment rose in Q2 2026, and youth (15–34) unemployment rose by 1.5 points: 33.6% overall; 47.4% youth; 5.0 million unemployed youth (SAnews/Stats SA, 11 Aug 2026) [AFW-19]
  - ILO-modelled unemployment for ages 15–24: 59.93% (World Bank/ILO, 2025) [AFW-30]
  - Remittances are negligible because South Africa is a destination country: 0.24% of GDP (World Bank, 2025) [AFW-29]
- **Migration & agencies:**
  - Scam recruiters are reportedly based in South Africa and Namibia. Job ads offered marketing, teaching or hospitality work in Thailand: R15,000/month (TimesLIVE, 29 Oct 2025) [AFW-10]
  - Earlier victims were recruited by an employment agency advertising on social media: (DIRCO, 27 Mar 2025) [AFW-9]
  - That private employment agencies must register with the Department of Employment and Labour under the Employment Services Act is UNVERIFIED (the official gov.za page refused connection).
- **Who is targeted:**
  - Young women aged 18–22 were targeted through Alabuga Start promotions on TikTok and Instagram (SAnews, 25 Aug 2025) [AFW-12]
  - Men aged 20–39, 16 of them from KwaZulu-Natal and 1 from the Eastern Cape, were recruited with promises of lucrative contracts: 17 men (SAnews, 6 Nov 2025) [AFW-14]
  - The Department of Social Development says victims are often forced to recruit friends and family (SAnews, 31 Jul 2026) [AFW-11]
- **Platforms & payments:**
  - Internet use: 51.7 million users (79.6%) (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [AFW-22]
  - TikTok reaches the most adults, followed by Facebook and YouTube: TikTok 29.1 million adults (64.8% of 18+); Facebook 27.9 million; YouTube 26.9 million; Instagram 8.6 million (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [AFW-22]
  - Recruiters for the Russian army used WhatsApp group chats: (Al Jazeera, 5 Mar 2026) [AFW-18]
- **Politics & elections 2026–27:**
  - Local government elections were proclaimed for 4 Nov 2026 (SAnews, 7 Aug 2026) [AFW-20]
  - The results were not checked in this pass.
- **Disinfo industry model:** Commercial PR and influencer marketing (Bell Pottinger; paid influencer promotion of Alabuga Start) [AFW-7][AFW-13]. South African nationals also helped run Russia's CAR network: ads paid mainly in rand and USD (Meta, 15 Dec 2020) [AFW-5]
- **Legal risks to victims:**
  - The Foreign Military Assistance Act (1998) bans South Africans from joining foreign armies without government authorisation (SAnews, 6 Nov 2025) [AFW-14]
  - Returnees from the Russian front are under police investigation under that law: 15 men (Al Jazeera, 5 Mar 2026) [AFW-18]
  - On return, scam-compound survivors were screened by Social Development and the Hawks (priority-crime police) (DIRCO, 27 Mar 2025) [AFW-9]
- **Consular reach:**
  - DIRCO used a bilateral anti-trafficking agreement with Thailand to bring victims home: 23 repatriated (DIRCO, 27 Mar 2025) [AFW-9]
  - Later escapees relied on an NGO (Brave to Love) to raise money for flights, food, documents and shelter: 36–41 people (TimesLIVE, 29 Oct 2025) [AFW-10]

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **Bell Pottinger (2016–2017).**
  - The UK PR firm ran an "economic emancipation" campaign for Oakbay Capital, the Gupta family's company. It cast the Guptas as fighting "white monopoly capital" and "economic apartheid" (Daily Maverick, 5 Sep 2017) [AFW-8]
  - The Public Relations and Communications Association (PRCA) unanimously found the campaign likely to inflame racial discord and expelled the firm: barred for at least 5 years (Press Gazette, 5 Sep 2017) [AFW-7]
  - CEO James Henderson resigned [AFW-7]
  - The Herbert Smith Freehills review found the firm did not invent the "white monopoly capital" phrase (Daily Maverick, 5 Sep 2017) [AFW-8]
  - The widely reported fee of about £100,000 a month is UNVERIFIED (no source opened).
- **South Africans in Russia's CAR operation.**
  - Meta's Dec 2020 takedown found clusters relying on local nationals from CAR and South Africa: 61 Facebook accounts, 29 Pages, 7 Groups; ~263,000 Page followers; ~$38,000 in ads paid mainly in rand and USD (Meta, 15 Dec 2020) [AFW-5]
- **Paid influencer promotion of Alabuga Start (Aug 2025).**
  - Influencers posted about the programme. DJ Cyan Boujee said she was contracted to make videos, then said she had not been paid after refusing to post the rest: at least 10 influencers (ADF Magazine, 7 Oct 2025) [AFW-13]
  - Government pushback is in (c) below.

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- **Myanmar compounds (2025).**
  - Victims were lured by an agency's social-media ads promising well-paid jobs in Thailand with free accommodation and travel, then taken to Myanmar against their will: 23 South Africans (DIRCO, 27 Mar 2025) [AFW-9]
  - They spent more than 4 months under 24-hour armed guard, working 16-hour days, with beatings and contaminated water. A ransom was demanded: about R50,000 (DIRCO, 27 Mar 2025) [AFW-9]
  - In October 2025 a further group escaped KK Park after being recruited with Thailand jobs paying R15,000/month: 36–41 people (TimesLIVE, 29 Oct 2025) [AFW-10]
  - The Department of Social Development's 2026 warning says several groups have been rescued since 2024 (no total given) (SAnews, 31 Jul 2026) [AFW-11]
- **Russian army (2025–2026).**
  - The men believed they were going for VIP bodyguard/security training. Some were told they would be smuggled into Western Europe if they fought (Al Jazeera, 5 Mar 2026) [AFW-18]
  - Each received a lump sum at the start of their contract and was told to burn belongings and documents: R80,000 (~$4,800) (Al Jazeera, 5 Mar 2026) [AFW-18]
  - An unnamed spokesperson alleges Wagner paid at least R14 million for the group [AFW-18]
  - Returns: 4 men first, then 11 on 25 Feb 2026, with 2 left in Russia severely injured (Africanews, 25 Feb 2026; Arab News/AP, 26 Feb 2026) [AFW-16][AFW-17]
  - Two other South Africans, recruited through a separate scheme, died: 2 deaths (Arab News/AP, 26 Feb 2026) [AFW-17]
  - Duduzile Zuma-Sambudla (Jacob Zuma's daughter) denies wrongdoing, resigned as an MP, and is under police investigation. AP says she resigned in November 2025; Al Jazeera says December 2025 [AFW-17][AFW-18]
  - In a separate case, a SAFM radio presenter (Nonkululeko Mantula) and four men were arrested and remanded. Some were stopped at the airport trying to fly to Russia via the UAE: 5 arrested (Africanews, 2 Dec 2025) [AFW-15]
- **Alabuga Start (2025).**
  - DIRCO warned about unverified foreign job offers promoted by influencers, aimed especially at young women. The programme lists seven tracks: catering, service & hospitality, production operator, driver, installation, finishing work and logistics: women aged 18–22 (SAnews, 25 Aug 2025) [AFW-12]
  - A government security cluster investigated a separate influencer video promoting a two-year programme [AFW-12]
  - One South African woman was repatriated after contacting the embassy in Moscow: 1 (ADF Magazine, 7 Oct 2025) [AFW-13]

### 2.4 Senegal

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money:**
  - Youth unemployment: 3.62% (World Bank/ILO modelled, 2025) [AFW-31]
  - Vulnerable employment: 63.0% (World Bank/ILO, 2025) [AFW-32]
  - Remittances are the highest share of GDP in this sub-region: 10.64% of GDP (World Bank, 2023; 2024–25 not reported) [AFW-29]
- **Migration & agencies:**
  - The Atlantic pirogue route to the Canary Islands is a major emigration channel, but 2025–26 arrival figures were not verified in this pass (see section 4).

## 3. Synthesis for job seekers in West, Southern & Francophone Africa

### 3.1 What to know
- **"Customer care in Thailand" is the number-one trap.** Ads for "customer care", "marketing", "teaching" or "hospitality" jobs in Thailand with free flights and housing ($1,500 or R15,000 a month) have led Nigerians and South Africans into locked scam compounds in Myanmar [AFW-9][AFW-10][AFW-36]. If anyone tells you to say you are a "tourist" going to Mae Sot, or a driver meets you at Bangkok airport and drives you for hours, you are being trafficked.
- **Interview on Telegram plus "show money" is a red flag.** Real employers do not move interviews to Telegram, pay money into your account so you pass a visa check, or ask for a typing-test video and your bank statements before a contract [AFW-36]. Recruiters may also be friends or family who are themselves being forced to recruit [AFW-11].
- **A "scholarship", "work-study" or "training" in Russia can be a factory or an army contract.** Alabuga Start (women 18–22; "hospitality", "catering", "production operator"), "VIP bodyguard/security training" and "scholarships" have led to drone assembly lines or the front line [AFW-12][AFW-18][AFW-38][AFW-61]. Never sign a contract you cannot read, and never hand over your passport or burn your documents. Ghana counts about 55 dead out of 272 lured [AFW-40].
- **An influencer's post is not proof a job is real.** At least 10 South African influencers promoted Alabuga Start, and at least one was contracted to do so [AFW-13]. Check the offer with DIRCO or your embassy, not with the person advertising it [AFW-9][AFW-12].
- **"Social media manager" jobs can be propaganda jobs.** If a "social media" or "content" job asks you to post about another country's politics, run several accounts, post false stories about rivals, or hand your account passwords to a boss, it is an influence operation. EBLA workers were paid bonuses as their accounts grew; Nigerian influencers were paid in cash by follower count [AFW-1][AFW-2]. Workers have faced armed raids and interrogation [AFW-2], and election-season demand peaks before Nigeria's 16 Jan 2027 vote [AFW-33].
- **Check the licence before you pay or travel.**
  - Nigeria: the recruiter must hold a Federal Ministry of Labour licence for international placement; unlicensed recruiters who charge fees are likely fraudulent [AFW-39].
  - Ghana: check the MELR list or ask the Labour Department [AFW-41].
  - South Africa: call DIRCO (+27 12 351 1000) and register on Travel Smart [AFW-9].
- **You can be treated as an offender even when you are the victim.** Escapees have been charged with illegal entry in Thailand [AFW-36]. Fighting for a foreign army is a crime in South Africa [AFW-14][AFW-18], and people who chat with victims for a scam centre can be arrested at home [AFW-34]. Keep evidence of how you were recruited; it helps prove you were deceived.
- **Before you go, leave a trail.** Give family the employer's name, address and contacts; agree a check-in schedule; and save the numbers for your country. South Africa: SAPS 10111, Crime Stop 08600 10111, trafficking hotline 0800 222 777. Senegal: police 17. Côte d'Ivoire: police 170/110/111 [AFW-9][AFW-21][AFW-45][AFW-48].
