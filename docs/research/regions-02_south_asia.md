- **South Asia is mainly a region that sends people out, and it feeds two pipelines at once: Southeast Asian scam compounds and the Russian army.** India alone reported 6,720 citizens rescued from compounds in Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar (Rajya Sabha reply reported 11 Dec 2025) [SA-1]. As of late Aug 2026, it counted 227 citizens recruited into Russia's army, 51 of them dead [SA-9]. Nepal (118 killed, 132 missing in Russia's war) [SA-51] and Sri Lanka (59 dead out of 554 recruits) [SA-47] carry the heaviest Russia losses relative to population (an inference from population size). The pitch is the same in both pipelines: a non-combat or office job, high pay, and a free ticket or visa.
- **Remittance dependence makes going abroad a normal household plan, and agents are trusted.** Remittances were 32.3% of GDP in Nepal in 2025, up from 26.0% in 2024. The figure was 9.9% for Pakistan, 7.6% for Sri Lanka, 7.4% for Bangladesh and 3.8% for India (World Bank WDI, updated 8 Oct 2026) [SA-18]. In Nepal, a job offer from abroad meets almost no social scepticism.
- **The jobless-youth problem differs from country to country.** ILO-modelled youth unemployment (ages 15–24) in 2025 was highest in Nepal (20.6%), Sri Lanka (18.6%) and India (16.0%). It was lower in Bangladesh (9.4%) and Pakistan (9.6%) [SA-19]. Pakistan, however, has the region's highest share of young people not in employment, education or training: 28.5% in 2025, against 25.6% in India [SA-21]. Scam recruiters look for exactly these people: young, digitally literate, under-employed graduates.
- **Platforms differ by country.** Facebook is the main mass channel in Bangladesh, Nepal and Sri Lanka. TikTok's ad reach is very large in Pakistan, at 79.9M adults, or 54.6% of those aged 18+. India's largest measured platforms are YouTube, Instagram and Facebook [SA-62–SA-66]. India's government says unregistered agents advertise through Facebook, WhatsApp and text messages, and charge Rs 2–5 lakh [SA-16]. When Nepal banned 26 platforms in Sept 2025, the list included WhatsApp, Messenger, IMO, Line and Signal, which shows which apps people there actually use [SA-56].
- **Who runs disinformation varies.**
  - India and Pakistan: the Oxford Internet Institute rates both as "high capacity" countries with permanent cyber-troop teams. India's are centralised and party-driven; Pakistan's are decentralised and state/military-linked [SA-23].
  - Bangladesh: networks linked to the ruling party were taken down by Meta in 2018 and 2024 [SA-28][SA-29]. The country also has an open commercial market in fake engagement, paid through bKash, Nagad and Upay [SA-30].
  - Sri Lanka: rated "medium capacity" [SA-23].
  - Nepal: does not appear in that inventory, and organised paid trolling there is poorly documented.
- **Legal and verification systems exist on paper but are easy to go around.** Each country has a regulator with a licensed-agent list: eMigrate, BMET, BE&OE, SLBFE and DoFE (section 2). Scam recruiters avoid them by sending people on tourist or visa-on-arrival entries. India's embassy in Bangkok stresses that visa-free entry to Thailand does not allow work [SA-4]. Some Bangladeshi victims left home *with* official clearance for jobs in Cambodia and Laos, and were then moved through Thailand into Myanmar [SA-34].
- **Victims face legal risk.** People who fled the KK Park compound were held by Thai authorities for immigration violations [SA-4]. Indian returnees were expected to be questioned by investigators [SA-6]. India's government told its Supreme Court that many Russia recruits had signed "voluntary contracts" [SA-11]. Cambodia detained Pakistanis after a compound raid and released them as a "goodwill gesture" without charges [SA-41]. In each case, a victim can easily be treated as a suspect or an irregular migrant.

## 2. Country profiles

### 2.1 India

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money**
  - Youth (15–29) unemployment, usual status: 9.9% in 2025, down from 10.3% in 2024. Rural youth 8.3%, urban youth 13.6% (MoSPI/NSO PLFS Annual Report press note, 27 Mar 2026) [SA-17].
  - ILO-modelled youth (15–24) unemployment: 16.0% in 2025. Youth NEET share: 25.6% in 2025 (World Bank WDI, updated 8 Oct 2026) [SA-19][SA-21].
  - Informality proxy: only 23.6% of workers held regular wage or salaried jobs in 2025, while 56.2% were self-employed (MoSPI, 27 Mar 2026) [SA-17].
  - Female labour force participation (15+): 40.0% in 2025 (MoSPI, 27 Mar 2026) [SA-17].
  - Remittances: 3.8% of GDP in 2025 (World Bank WDI) [SA-18].
- **Migration & agencies**
  - Holders of passports marked ECR ("Emigration Check Required") need emigration clearance from a Protector of Emigrants (POE) to work in the listed ECR countries. The ministry's FAQ page gives the count as 17 in one place and 18 in another (MEA FAQ, undated) [SA-15].
  - India's government warns that unregistered agents recruit for Eastern Europe, some Gulf states, Central Asia, Israel, Canada, Myanmar and Laos. They advertise on Facebook, WhatsApp and SMS, give little contact detail, and charge Rs 2–5 lakh. A genuine offer comes with a signed contract and a work visa, not a tourist visa (MEA advisory dated 14 Dec 2023, republished by the Embassy of India, Bern) [SA-16].
- **Who is targeted**
  - Mostly men. Of the 270 citizens flown home from Mae Sot on 6 Nov 2025, 26 were women (ThePrint, 6 Nov 2025) [SA-4].
  - Advertised roles assume office and computer skills: "Digital Sales and Marketing Executive" and "Customer Support Service" (MEA advisory via News On AIR, 17 May 2024) [SA-13].
  - Internet use: 70% of the population in 2025 (World Bank WDI) [SA-20]; 1.03 billion users at end-2025 (DataReportal, 5 Nov 2025) [SA-62].
- **Platforms & payments**
  - Ad reach in late 2025 (DataReportal, 5 Nov 2025) [SA-62]:
    - YouTube: 500M
    - Instagram: 481M
    - Facebook: 403M
    - Snapchat: 213M
    - Messenger: 108M
  - WhatsApp has no DataReportal figure, but government advisories name it as an agent channel [SA-16].
  - UPI volumes and the 2020 TikTok ban: primary sources could not be opened, so UNVERIFIED.
- **Politics & elections 2026–27**
  - Five assembly elections were counted on 4–5 May 2026 (India TV, 5 May 2026; the article gives the BJP's West Bengal total as 206 in its text and 207 in its table) [SA-67]:
    - West Bengal: the BJP won 207 of 294 seats, ending the TMC's rule.
    - Tamil Nadu: TVK won 108 of 234, short of a majority.
    - Kerala: the Congress-led UDF won a decisive mandate.
    - Assam: the BJP won 82 of 126.
    - Puducherry: AINRC led with 12 of 30.
  - 2027: assembly elections are expected in several large states (e.g., Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Gujarat). This is UNVERIFIED, inferred from the five-year terms of assemblies elected in 2022.
- **Disinfo industry model**
  - Mostly run by parties, with large volunteer and worker layers and some contractors. The Oxford Internet Institute rates India's cyber troops as high capacity, permanent and centralised (OII, 13 Jan 2021) [SA-23].
  - In 2019 Meta removed assets linked to both the Congress party's IT Cell and to Silver Touch, an IT firm whose page was pro-BJP (Meta, 1 Apr 2019) [SA-22] (see b).
- **Legal risks to victims**
  - People who fled KK Park into Thailand were held by Thai authorities for immigration violations (ThePrint, 6 Nov 2025) [SA-4]. Reports said Indian agencies would question returnees about how the compound operated (Millennium Post/agencies, 6 Nov 2025) [SA-6].
  - On Russia, the government told the Supreme Court that agents misled many recruits, but that many had also signed voluntary contracts with Russian entities (Scroll, 24 Apr 2026) [SA-11]. That framing could weaken recruits' claim to be treated as victims.
- **Consular reach**
  - Relatively strong. The government used Indian Air Force aircraft for mass evacuations: 549 people in March 2025 [SA-3], and 270 on 6 Nov plus 125 on 19 Nov 2025 [SA-4][SA-5].
  - Missions offer walk-in help, email, 24x7 numbers, WhatsApp and the MADAD, CPGRAMS and eMigrate grievance portals (ThePrint/PTI, 11 Dec 2025) [SA-1].
  - The government says it does not know how many citizens are still stranded in the compounds [SA-1][SA-2].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **Meta takedown, April 2019: Congress IT Cell.** Meta removed 687 Pages and accounts (138 Pages, 549 accounts) linked to individuals in the Indian National Congress IT Cell. About 206,000 accounts followed them, and they had spent about US$39,000 on ads between Aug 2014 and Mar 2019 (Meta, 1 Apr 2019) [SA-22].
- **The same takedown: Silver Touch.** Meta removed 15 assets linked to Silver Touch, an Indian IT firm. Its main Page had about 2.6M followers, and the network spent about US$70,000 on ads between Jun 2014 and Feb 2019 (Meta, 1 Apr 2019) [SA-22]. *Why it matters:* a commercial tech vendor knowingly ran political assets.
- **BJP IT Cell structure (secondary source only).**
  - Wikipedia, citing The Washington Post (27 Sep 2023), describes about 150,000 campaign workers pushing content through WhatsApp groups.
  - It cites Newslaundry (17 Mar 2017) for an Uttar Pradesh structure of about 5,000 workers across regional, district and assembly levels.
  - Its infobox estimates 5,000–6,000 employees [SA-25].
  - The underlying articles were not opened (UNVERIFIED beyond Wikipedia). None of the sources opened says how many of these workers are paid and how many are volunteers.
- **Retracted reporting: do not use as evidence.** In January 2022, The Wire published "Tek Fog", an investigation alleging that BJP-linked operators used an app to hijack trends and harass critics. The Wire removed the series on 23 Oct 2022 pending an internal review, after a co-author's separate Meta reporting was found to involve deception. It apologised on 27 Oct 2022. In Feb 2022 the government had said it could not find the app (Wikipedia "Tek Fog") [SA-24].
- **Did workers know?** Party volunteers and contractors know they are promoting a party; the documented cases do not show deceived job-seekers. There is no verified evidence in this pass of Indian job ads that disguise political trolling as "social media jobs". This is a gap.

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- **Totals**
  - Rescued from compounds in Cambodia, Lao PDR and Myanmar: more than 6,700, namely Cambodia 2,265, Lao PDR 2,290 and Myanmar 2,165 (written Rajya Sabha reply by Minister of State Kirti Vardhan Singh; ThePrint/PTI, 11 Dec 2025) [SA-1].
  - A later reply put the 2022–2025 total at 6,998: Cambodia 2,533, Laos 2,297, Myanmar 2,168 (Scroll, 6 Feb 2026) [SA-2].
  - The two replies give different per-country splits. The government says the number still stranded is unknown [SA-2].
- **How people were recruited**
  - Dubious firms posted fake recruitment offers, mostly on social media, and people travelled through unscrupulous agents and illegal channels (ThePrint/PTI, 11 Dec 2025) [SA-1].
  - Indian embassies warned in May 2024 about fake openings for "Digital Sales and Marketing Executives" and "Customer Support Service". The jobs were in Laos's Golden Triangle Special Economic Zone and in Cambodia, and recruits were forced into call-centre and crypto fraud (News On AIR, 17 May 2024; India TV, 17 May 2024) [SA-13][SA-14].
  - A visa on arrival in Thailand or Laos does not permit work [SA-14].
  - One Indian man in the 2025 Myawaddy camps said he had applied for a "data entry" job in Thailand (AFP via Arab News, 27 Feb 2025) [SA-39].
- **February–March 2025 crackdown**
  - 549 Indians were flown home from Mae Sot on two Indian Air Force flights: 283 on 10 Mar and 266 on 11 Mar 2025 (Scroll, 12 Mar 2025) [SA-3].
- **October–November 2025 (after the KK Park raid)**
  - 270 were flown home on 6 Nov 2025, including 26 women (ThePrint, 6 Nov 2025) [SA-4].
  - About 500 Indians had crossed into Thailand (Millennium Post, 6 Nov 2025) [SA-6].
  - A further 125 were flown home on 19 Nov 2025. By then, the embassy said, 1,500 Indians released from Myawaddy compounds had been repatriated through Thailand since March 2025 (IANS, 19 Nov 2025) [SA-5].
  - A second batch reported as 197 was seen in search snippets only (UNVERIFIED).
- **Russian army**
  - Dec 2025: 202 Indians recruited since 2022, 26 killed, 7 missing, 119 discharged and 50 still awaiting discharge. Ten bodies had been repatriated and 2 cremated in Russia (Rajya Sabha reply; IANS, 18 Dec 2025) [SA-7]. Scroll reported the same reply as 206 recruited [SA-11].
  - Apr 2026: the government told the Supreme Court that 10 of the 26 men whose families had petitioned were dead. It said agents duped people into support roles such as cooks and helpers, and that Russia had reportedly stopped recruiting Indians in April 2024 (Scroll, 24 Apr 2026) [SA-11].
  - 31 Jul 2026: 139 released, about two dozen still in the army (MEA spokesperson; IANS, 31 Jul 2026) [SA-8].
  - Late Aug 2026: Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri said 227 recruited, 139 discharged and 51 dead (briefing in Bishkek; Free Press Journal, 1 Sep 2026; NewKerala, 1 Sep 2026) [SA-9][SA-10].
  - Note: the death toll roughly doubled from 26 (Dec 2025) to 51 (late Aug 2026). The July briefing gave only "over two dozen" dead.
- **Russia lure details**
  - May 2024: the CBI arrested four people, including a translator on contract to Russia's Defence Ministry. Its March statement had counted about 35 men duped.
  - Recruits were offered lucrative jobs or university places. Recruits told Al Jazeera they were promised high salaries and Russian passports, and the families of two dead men said they expected "helper" jobs (Al Jazeera, 8 May 2024) [SA-12].

### 2.2 Bangladesh

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money**
  - ILO-modelled youth unemployment: 9.4% in 2025. Youth NEET: 15.4% in 2024 (World Bank WDI) [SA-19][SA-21].
  - Remittances: 7.4% of GDP in 2025, up from 6.1% in 2024 (World Bank WDI) [SA-18].
  - Migration-cost debt: a national figure was not verified in this pass. The AP's reported fee of 1.2 million taka for a Russia "job" was seen in a snippet only (UNVERIFIED).
- **Migration & agencies**
  - BAIRA, the association of recruiting agencies, represents about 2,500 government-approved agencies (BAIRA site) [SA-38].
  - Victims reached the compounds by more than one route. In an Oct 2026 case, men first went to Cambodia and Laos with official overseas-employment clearance. Traffickers then moved them to Thailand with promises of better jobs and forced them into Myanmar at gunpoint (bdnews24, 21 Oct 2026) [SA-34].
  - Others were promised "computer-related" jobs in Dubai, Malaysia or Thailand (bdnews24, 23 Jan 2026) [SA-35].
- **Who is targeted**
  - Young men with basic computer skills. Advertised roles included computer operator, call-centre executive and customer-service staff, posted through fake websites, email and social-media ads [SA-34].
  - Internet use: 47.0% of the population at end-2025 per DataReportal [SA-63]; 53.4% in 2024 per the World Bank [SA-20].
- **Platforms & payments**
  - Ad reach in late 2025 (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [SA-63]:
    - Facebook: 64.0M (36.3% of the population)
    - TikTok: 56.2M adults
    - YouTube: 49.8M
    - Messenger: 35.0M
  - The commercial engagement market is paid through bKash, Nagad and Upay (Daily Star via ANN/The Star, 5 Feb 2026) [SA-30].
  - imo usage: no data found (gap).
- **Politics & elections 2026–27**
  - In the 12 Feb 2026 election, the BNP won 209 seats in the unofficial count and Jamaat-e-Islami 68, its best result ever. The Awami League was barred, turnout was almost 60%, and a reform referendum was held alongside (Al Jazeera, 13 Feb 2026) [SA-31].
  - Tarique Rahman was expected to become prime minister [SA-31]. That he was sworn in is UNVERIFIED in this pass.
- **Disinfo industry model:** a hybrid. Networks have been linked to the ruling party (see b), and there is a large commercial market in bots and engagement open to any buyer [SA-30].
- **Legal risks to victims**
  - Five rescued Bangladeshis were still in a Thai immigration safe house in Mae Sot while legal procedures continued (bdnews24, 21 Oct 2026) [SA-34].
  - Families pay ransoms: US$10,000 in one case [SA-34].
- **Consular reach**
  - Thin and often driven by NGOs. Rescues went through the Bangkok embassy's labour wing after the Wage Earners' Welfare Board was approached by the Anirban Survivor Voice Foundation (bdnews24, 21 Oct 2026) [SA-34], and through BRAC [SA-35].
  - About 50 Bangladeshis had been repatriated from compounds in the Thai–Myanmar border area as of 28 Apr 2026 (Bangladesh Monitor, citing an embassy post) [SA-36].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **Meta, Dec 2018.** Meta removed 9 Pages and 6 accounts made to look like independent news sites, posting pro-government and anti-opposition content. It linked them to individuals associated with the Bangladesh government. About 11,900 followers; about US$800 in ads (Meta, 20 Dec 2018) [SA-28].
- **Meta, Q1 2024.** Meta removed 50 Facebook accounts and 98 Pages linked to individuals associated with the Awami League and the Centre for Research and Information (CRI), a non-profit. About 3.4M accounts followed the Pages. Some posed as news outlets, and some used the BNP's name to post anti-BNP content (The Daily Star, 30 May 2024) [SA-29].
- **Commercial click farms around the Feb 2026 election.** The Daily Star analysed 263 political posts with 885,811 reactions, of which 62% were "haha". It found at least 19,708 "haha" reactions from bot-like profiles. It bought about 30,000 reactions itself from click farms named Sociafy, Finix IT, Tech Dreams, Finix IT Boosting and Fatherpanel.com. Prices were 120–200 taka per 1,000 reactions, and Bangladeshi bot accounts sold for about 15–16 taka each, paid via bKash, Nagad or Upay. Clusters aligned with Jamaat, the Awami League and the BNP attacked rivals and media outlets (Daily Star via The Star/ANN, 5 Feb 2026) [SA-30].
- **Did workers know?** Click-farm operators sell engagement openly as a service. No documented case was found in this pass of job-seekers recruited without knowing the work was political.

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- **Scam compounds**
  - Repatriations: 18 on 19 Sep 2025, 8 on 22 Jan 2026, and 3 more due in Oct 2026, with 5 still waiting in Thailand (bdnews24, 23 Jan 2026 and 21 Oct 2026) [SA-34][SA-35]. About 50 in total from the border-area operations as of Apr 2026 [SA-36].
  - Abuse described: passports and phones taken; punishment for missing weekly targets of harvested phone numbers (collected via Facebook), including confinement in dark rooms; one-year to 18-month "contracts" [SA-35]; a torture room workers called the "black room" [SA-34].
- **Russian army**
  - An AP investigation (Jan 2026) found Bangladeshi men lured with promises of civilian work. In Moscow they were made to sign Russian-language papers that turned out to be military contracts. They were then trained in drone warfare, medical evacuation and heavy weapons, and forced into front-line tasks (AP via Macau Daily Times, 28 Jan 2026) [SA-32].
  - BRAC found at least 10 Bangladeshi men still missing; one survivor had been promised janitor work (BRAC USA excerpt of AP, 27 Jan 2026) [SA-33].
  - AP's figures of about 40 dead, the agency name "SP Global" and the 1.2 million taka fee were seen in snippets only (UNVERIFIED).

### 2.3 Pakistan

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money**
  - ILO-modelled youth unemployment: 9.6% in 2025. Youth NEET: 28.5% in 2025, the highest of the five countries (World Bank WDI) [SA-19][SA-21].
  - Remittances: 9.9% of GDP in 2025 (World Bank WDI) [SA-18].
- **Migration & agencies**
  - Overseas Employment Promoters (OEPs) are licensed by the Bureau of Emigration & Overseas Employment (BE&OE), which sits under the Ministry of Overseas Pakistanis & HRD (ministry site) [SA-44].
  - An older snapshot shows how weak licence hygiene is. In June 2022, of 4,685 registered OEPs, 2,358 were valid, 1,256 expired, 172 cancelled, 148 invalid and 724 surrendered (Aaj TV, 29 Jun 2022) [SA-68].
- **Who is targeted**
  - Thai officials said traffickers used promised jobs, business deals or relationships as bait (Daily Times, 3 Dec 2025) [SA-40].
  - Internet use: 45.6% of the population [SA-64]; 57.3% in 2024 per the World Bank [SA-20].
- **Platforms & payments**
  - Ad reach in late 2025 (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [SA-64]:
    - TikTok: 79.9M adults (54.6% of those aged 18+)
    - YouTube: 54.3M
    - Facebook: 52.9M
    - Instagram: 22.4M
  - JazzCash and Easypaisa: usage figures not verified (gap).
- **Politics & elections 2026–27:** no national election is believed to be due before 2029. UNVERIFIED in this pass.
- **Disinfo industry model:** mainly state- and military-linked, with party teams and PR firms (see b). OII rates Pakistan high capacity, with permanent, decentralised teams, and singles it out for human networks that mass-report opposing posts to get them removed (OII, 13 Jan 2021) [SA-23].
- **Legal risks to victims**
  - In April 2026 Cambodia detained 54 Pakistanis after a raid on a scam compound in Siem Reap. More than 200 Pakistanis were reported to be in Cambodian police custody. Cambodia agreed to send the 54 home as a "goodwill gesture" without legal proceedings, and the Foreign Office called them detainees, not victims (Dawn, 28 Apr 2026) [SA-41].
  - Thai officials urged Pakistan to investigate how the citizens had been trafficked [SA-40].
- **Consular reach**
  - In Feb 2025 a Pakistani held in a Myawaddy camp said he felt abandoned. The Karen Border Guard Force urged embassies to collect their nationals (AFP via Arab News, 27 Feb 2025) [SA-39].
  - In 2026 the Foreign Office said its embassy in Cambodia had pursued the detainees' release [SA-41].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **Meta, Apr 2019: military-linked network.** Meta removed 103 assets (24 Pages, 57 accounts, 7 Groups, 15 Instagram accounts) linked to employees of the ISPR, the military's media wing. About 2.8M accounts followed the Pages (Meta, 1 Apr 2019) [SA-22].
- **Meta, Aug 2020: mass-reporting network.** The largest network in that month's report had 453 Facebook accounts, 103 Pages, 78 Groups and 107 Instagram accounts. It urged users to mass-report critics, including Ahmadi accounts, and praised the military, the ISI and the PTI party. The Stanford Internet Observatory said it used a browser extension to automate reports (Al Jazeera, 2 Sep 2020) [SA-26].
- **Meta, May 2021: PR firm.** Meta removed 40 Facebook accounts, 25 Pages, 6 Groups and 28 Instagram accounts linked to individuals associated with AlphaPro, a Pakistan-based PR firm. The network targeted Pakistani audiences and English-, Arabic- and Pashto-speaking audiences (Meta, 3 Jun 2021) [SA-27]. This is the clearest *commercial* case found.
- **"Beyond Digital Solutions" (named in the brief):** no takedown naming this firm could be found (UNVERIFIED; possibly a different name).
- **Did workers know?** The documented networks were run by staff of state media units and a PR firm. No verified case was found of job-seekers deceived into this work.

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- **Feb 2025:** 14 Pakistani men were among the detainees waiting in an overcrowded camp in Myawaddy. Bosses had taken their passports and phones (AFP via Arab News, 27 Feb 2025) [SA-39].
- **Dec 2025:** Thai authorities took in 38 Pakistanis who had crossed the border, and 60 more were in a shelter awaiting identity checks (Daily Times, 3 Dec 2025) [SA-40].
- **Apr 2026:** 54 detained in Siem Reap, Cambodia, with more than 200 reportedly in Cambodian custody (Dawn, 28 Apr 2026) [SA-41].
- **Snippet-only figures (UNVERIFIED):** 21 victims returning in April 2025, and "500 Pakistanis held captive" in March 2025 (The News; pages blocked).
- **Russian army:** no verified Pakistan-specific figures found.

## 3. Synthesis for job seekers in South Asia

### 3.1 What to know
- **Check the agent before you pay anything.** Every country here has a free official list:
  - India: eMigrate, "List of active RA"
  - Bangladesh: the BMET agency list
  - Pakistan: the BE&OE list of licensed OEPs
  - Sri Lanka: the SLBFE list of valid licensed agencies
  - Nepal: DoFE's foreignjob portal

  If the agent is not listed, or will only talk on WhatsApp or Facebook, walk away. India's government says unregistered agents work through Facebook, WhatsApp and SMS and charge Rs 2–5 lakh [SA-16]. In Nepal the legal maximum fee for the main Gulf and Malaysia jobs is Rs10,000 [SA-57].
- **A tourist visa or visa-on-arrival is never a work permit.** India's embassy warns that visa-free entry to Thailand is only for tourism or short business trips [SA-4][SA-5], and a visa on arrival in Thailand or Laos does not permit work [SA-14]. If your "employer" flies you to Bangkok and then drives you to a border town such as Mae Sot, you are being trafficked.
- **"Office jobs" in Thailand, Cambodia, Laos or Myanmar with big salaries and no real interview are the classic scam-compound lure.** The job titles actually used include:
  - "Digital Sales and Marketing Executive" and "Customer Support Service" [SA-13]
  - "data entry" [SA-39]
  - "IT specialist", "digital marketer" and "customer service representative" [SA-50]
  - "computer operator" and "call centre executive" [SA-34]

  Thousands from this region have been rescued, including about 7,000 Indians since 2022 [SA-2], and some families have paid ransoms of US$10,000 [SA-34].
- **Be very wary of "helper", "cook", "cleaner", "security guard", "logistics" or "student" offers for Russia, especially if a Russian passport is promised.** Indians, Nepalis, Sri Lankans and Bangladeshis who took such offers ended up in combat [SA-11][SA-12][SA-32][SA-47][SA-51]. Never sign a document you cannot read: Bangladeshi men signed Russian-language papers that turned out to be army contracts [SA-32]. Nepal bans work travel to Russia and Ukraine altogether [SA-52].
- **"Earn from home by liking, commenting or posting for a page" can be political manipulation.** In Bangladesh, click farms sell "haha" reactions and bot accounts for political attacks, paid through bKash and Nagad [SA-30]. Parties and state agencies in India and Pakistan run permanent online teams [SA-22][SA-23][SA-26]. Ask who the client is. Refuse jobs that require fake accounts, mass-reporting other people's posts, or posting content you are not allowed to check.
- **Before you leave, save the numbers.** In Nepal:
  - Police: 100
  - Anti-trafficking bureau: 1177
  - Cyber Bureau: 01-5319044
  - DoFE: 01-4792671

  Pakistan's numbers (FIA trafficking hotline 111-247-786, NCCIA cyber helpline 1799) are reported by the news media but were not confirmed on official sites; see 2.3d. Register your trip with the labour ministry or bureau so the embassy can find you.
- **If you are trapped, you are a victim, but keep evidence.** Embassies and the IOM do repatriate people [SA-3][SA-45]. You may still be held for immigration offences or questioned at home [SA-4][SA-6][SA-41]. Keep screenshots of the job ad, chats and payment receipts. They show you were deceived.
