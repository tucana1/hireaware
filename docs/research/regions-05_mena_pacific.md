# Middle East and North Africa (MENA)

Scope: Egypt, Tunisia, Morocco and Jordan, plus two firms (Israel's Archimedes Group and Tunisia's UReputation) that ran influence operations aimed at African countries. All pages were opened on 2026-11-07.

Research limits for this section:
- The shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so later checks relied only on URLs already found.
- Several official sites could not be opened from our fetcher: ANAPEC, ATCT, the Moroccan and Tunisian employment ministries, Egypt's Ministry of Labour (TLS error), NCCM, NCW and NCCPIM&TIP. They either refused the connection, failed TLS or did not resolve.
- The US State Department's 2025 Trafficking in Persons (TIP) country pages returned HTTP 403.
- Claims that depend on any of these are marked UNVERIFIED.

## 1. Regional picture: what shapes the risk here

- **Morocco has the best-documented flow into scam compounds. Tunisia has a few cases. Egypt and Jordan have no public caseload that we could find.**
  - Morocco's Foreign Ministry identified 34 trafficked citizens: 24 in Myanmar, 5 in Laos and 5 in Cambodia. It later assisted 5 more in Cambodia, for a total of 39, and paid for 26 flights home (Hespress, 28 Sep 2024) [MENA-14].
  - Morocco then convicted a scam-compound recruiter for the first time (AP via Arab News, 5 Nov 2025) [MENA-12].
  - Tunisia repatriated 3 citizens held in "forced detention" in Myanmar (L'Economiste Maghrébin, 17 Apr 2025) [MENA-20].
  - We found no official Egyptian or Jordanian figures in sources we could open (gap).
- **North Africa has been a source of commercial influence-for-hire sold to clients abroad.**
  - Tunisia's PR firm UReputation ran more than 900 Facebook and Instagram assets aimed at elections in about 10 African countries, with about US$331,000 in ad spend (DFRLab, 5 Jun 2020) [MENA-2].
  - Egyptian marketing firms New Waves and Flexell were tied to cross-border networks that pushed Gulf-aligned messaging (Meta, Aug and Oct 2019) [MENA-5][MENA-6].
  - Israel's Archimedes Group targeted Nigeria, Senegal, Togo, Angola, Niger and Tunisia, with about US$812,000 in ad spend (Meta, 16 May 2019) [MENA-4].
  - By contrast, the documented networks in Morocco and Jordan were domestic and state-aligned, not commercial [MENA-8][MENA-9][MENA-10].
- **Youth job markets are weakest in Jordan and Tunisia.**
  - Youth unemployment (ages 15–24, ILO model, 2025): Jordan 38.9%, Tunisia 38.1%, Morocco 21.9%, Egypt 18.3% [MENA-21].
  - Share of young people not in education, employment or training (NEET): Jordan 29.4% (2024), Egypt 23.8% (2024), Tunisia 22.6% (2023). No recent Moroccan value is published in WDI [MENA-23].
- **Informal and precarious work is highest in Morocco and Egypt.** "Vulnerable employment" (ILO model, 2025) is 35.5% of all jobs in Morocco, 23.9% in Egypt, 17.9% in Tunisia and 7.9% in Jordan [MENA-24].
- **Households depend heavily on remittances.** Remittances as a share of GDP: Egypt 11.37% (2025, up from 4.93% in 2023), Jordan 7.56% (2024), Morocco 7.49% (2025), Tunisia 6.74% (2025) [MENA-22].
- **Connectivity is near-universal and TikTok reach is large, so online job ads reach almost everyone.**
  - Internet penetration at end-2025: Jordan 92.5%, Morocco 92.2%, Tunisia 84.3%, Egypt 82.7% [MENA-25][MENA-26][MENA-27][MENA-28].
  - TikTok ad reach covers 67.6% of Tunisian adults and 62.3% of Moroccan adults [MENA-27][MENA-26].
- **The documented lure is "a good job in Thailand".**
  - Advertised jobs: e-commerce, call-centre, casino or "digital-sector" work.
  - Route: flights often via Malaysia, then Bangkok, then the border town of Mae Sot, then by road across the river into Myanmar's Karen State.
  - Ransoms of US$6,000–7,000 [MENA-12][MENA-15][MENA-16][MENA-17][MENA-18].
  - Recruiters are often fellow nationals. The convicted Moroccan recruiter ran a Facebook group for Moroccan migrants in Türkiye and earned US$21–107 per recruit [MENA-12].
- **Consular reach in the Mekong region is thin.**
  - Moroccan cases were handled through the embassy in Bangkok, helped by an NGO and Thai authorities [MENA-16][MENA-17].
  - Tunisia's case was handled by staff sent from its embassy in Jakarta [MENA-20].

## 2. Country profiles

### 2.1 Egypt

#### a) Vulnerability factors

**Jobs & money**
- Youth unemployment was 18.3% in 2025, up from 16.3% in 2022 (World Bank WDI / ILO model, updated 8 Oct 2026) [MENA-21].
- NEET was 23.8% in 2024, down from 30.2% in 2020 [MENA-23].
- Vulnerable employment was 23.9% of total employment in 2025 [MENA-24].
- Remittances rose to 11.37% of GDP in 2025, from 4.93% in 2023 [MENA-22].
- Household debt: we found no figure (gap).

**Migration & agencies**
- Recruitment for jobs abroad goes through companies licensed by the Ministry of Labour.
- On 5 Nov 2025 the minister acted against 10 overseas-recruitment companies: 8 newly suspended (one still being set up), 1 suspension extended and 1 closed. Their licence numbers were published.
- The legal basis cited was Labour Law 12/2003. It allows licences to be revoked for unlawfully charging workers or submitting false information (Egypt Telegraph, 5 Nov 2025) [MENA-35].
- At a roundtable with the ILO, the labour minister said a new labour law enacted in 2025 regulates private recruitment agencies, and promised a clampdown on fraudulent recruiters (Amwal Al Ghad, 21 Sep 2025) [MENA-36].
- Main corridors (Gulf states, Jordan, Libya, Europe): UNVERIFIED in this pass.

**Who is targeted**
- We found no Egypt-specific profile of scam-compound victims (gap).
- Domestic recruitment fraud mostly uses fake "work abroad" ads that ask for upfront fees. The ministry's own enforcement statements describe this (see 2.1d) [MENA-35].

**Platforms & payments**
- Internet users: 98.2 million (82.7% of the population).
- Ad reach as of late 2025: Facebook 51.6 million, YouTube 49.3 million, TikTok 48.8 million adults, Instagram 21.7 million (DataReportal Digital 2026: Egypt, 8 Nov 2025) [MENA-25].
- WhatsApp and Telegram usage: no figure in the sources we opened (gap).
- InstaPay ("InstaPay by EBC") is an instant-transfer app that sends to bank accounts, wallets, cards or a mobile-number address. Its per-transaction limit is EGP 70,000 [MENA-38]. Usage figures for mobile wallets such as Vodafone Cash: UNVERIFIED.

**Politics & elections 2026–27**
- House of Representatives elections ran from Nov 2025 to Jan 2026.
- On 18 Nov 2025 the National Elections Authority (NEA) cancelled results in 19 of 70 constituencies. On 30 Nov the Supreme Administrative Court invalidated 28 more, so 47 were affected.
- Final results (10 Jan 2026): turnout 32.4%. Nation's Future won 227 of 568 elected seats, Homeland Defenders 87 and the National Front 65 (International IDEA Democracy Tracker) [MENA-30].
- The sources we opened show no national election scheduled for 2026–27.

**Disinformation industry model**
- It is mixed. One strand is operations run from a pro-government media outlet: Twitter removed 2,541 accounts linked to El Fagr in April 2020, and MediaNama reported they acted at the government's direction [MENA-7].
- The other strand is commercial marketing firms working for Gulf-aligned clients (New Waves, Flexell) [MENA-5][MENA-6].

**Legal risks to victims**
- Whether returning trafficking victims can be punished under Egypt's anti-trafficking and migrant-smuggling laws: UNVERIFIED. The TIP 2025 page and the legal texts were not reachable.

**Consular reach**
- Not verified. An Ahram Online report on a Foreign Ministry WhatsApp hotline for Egyptians abroad returned 403, so it is UNVERIFIED.

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment

- **New Waves (Egypt) / Newave (UAE).** Meta removed 259 Facebook accounts (200 already disabled), 102 Pages, 5 Groups, 4 events and 17 Instagram accounts (Meta, 1 Aug 2019) [MENA-5].
  - About 13.7 million accounts followed the Pages. Ad spend was about US$167,000, paid in US dollars and Emirati dirhams.
  - Targets: Libya, Sudan, Comoros, Qatar, Turkey, Lebanon, Syria, Jordan and Morocco.
  - Tactics: Pages posing as local news outlets, impersonation of public figures, and lifestyle content mixed with pro-UAE politics.
- **El Fagr–linked network.** Meta removed 163 Facebook accounts, 51 Pages, 33 Groups and 4 Instagram accounts (Meta, 3 Oct 2019) [MENA-6].
  - About 5.6 million Page followers and under US$31,000 in ads.
  - Targets: Somalia, Yemen, Saudi Arabia, Sudan, Tunisia, Iran, Turkey, Lebanon and Qatar.
  - Pages posed as independent local news, and some appear to have been bought.
- **Charles Communications (UAE), MintReach (Nigeria) and Flexell (Egypt).** Meta removed 211 Facebook accounts, 107 Pages, 43 Groups and 87 Instagram accounts, with under US$150,000 in ads (Meta, 3 Oct 2019) [MENA-6].
- **El Fagr on Twitter.** Twitter removed 2,541 accounts that had posted more than 7 million times (MediaNama, 3 Apr 2020) [MENA-7].
  - They attacked Iran, Qatar and Turkey, often over Turkey's role in Libya.
  - Handles mimicked news brands; one posed as a Fox News affiliate.
  - The Stanford Internet Observatory's count of 7,935,267 tweets and account creation dates between 2008 and 2019: UNVERIFIED (snippet only; page returned 403).
- **How workers were recruited and paid:** None of the sources we opened says how individual operators were hired or paid, or whether they knew the accounts were fake. The networks are attributed to the firms or the outlet as organisations (gap).

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment

- We found no Egyptian government figures on citizens trapped in Southeast Asian scam compounds, and no media reports of such cases, in sources we could open (gap).
- Egypt does have documented domestic overseas-recruitment fraud, shown by the November 2025 suspensions [MENA-35].
- Egyptians deceptively recruited into the Russian army: not researched (gap).

### 2.3 Morocco

#### a) Vulnerability factors

**Jobs & money**
- Youth unemployment was 21.9% in 2025 (ILO model) [MENA-21]. National statistics office (HCP) figures for ages 15–24 are reported to be much higher, but this is UNVERIFIED because the HCP figures were not opened.
- Vulnerable employment was 35.5% in 2025, the highest of the four countries [MENA-24].
- Remittances were 7.49% of GDP in 2025 [MENA-22].

**Migration & agencies**
- ANAPEC is the public employment agency that runs international placement, but its site refused connection (UNVERIFIED).
- The Morocco–Spain seasonal farm-work scheme: UNVERIFIED in this pass.
- Recruiters also target the diaspora. The convicted recruiter used a Facebook group that served Moroccan immigrants in Türkiye [MENA-12].

**Who is targeted**
- Prosecutors and media describe the victims as young Moroccans who answered online job offers [MENA-12][MENA-13]. Documented offers:
  - E-commerce jobs in Thailand paying US$1,000 at first, then US$2,000 [MENA-17].
  - Casino jobs [MENA-16].
  - "Digital-sector" jobs [MENA-15].
  - Recruiters used fake profiles or fake recruitment websites [MENA-18].
- Thai authorities warned about recruitment of Moroccan youth through attractive "training" offers or e-commerce jobs (Al3omk, 15 Jul 2024) [MENA-19].
- Gender breakdown: not reported (gap).

**Platforms & payments**
- Internet users: 35.5 million (92.2%).
- Ad reach as of late 2025: Facebook 22.8 million, YouTube 21.6 million, TikTok 16.7 million adults (62.3% of adults), Instagram 15.1 million [MENA-26].
- Ransoms were paid in cryptocurrency [MENA-12].

**Politics & elections 2026–27**
- The legislative election was held on 23 Sep 2026. Provisional results from Interior Minister Abdelouafi Laftit, out of 395 seats [MENA-29]:
  - PAM 97, RNI 66, Istiqlal 65, PJD 54, MP 29, USFP 26, PPS 19, UC 17.
  - Turnout was 38.08%. Some outlets report 38.02% (UNVERIFIED, snippet only).
- The status of government formation in November 2026 is UNVERIFIED.

**Disinformation industry model**
- The documented operations are domestic and state-aligned, aimed at journalists and critics. No commercial firm has been named [MENA-8][MENA-9].

**Legal risks to victims**
- Some Moroccans held in Myanmar refused to come home because they feared prosecution. Their families had filed trafficking complaints, and the Casablanca prosecutor had opened an investigation. One young man was suspected of having helped bring others in [MENA-19].
- In the 2024 case, four defendants were held on charges including organising illegal emigration, trafficking, torture and detention. Some said they were themselves recruited victims [MENA-15].
- One victim was told to pay a ransom or recruit 100 other people [MENA-12]. Victims pushed into recruiting can end up treated as traffickers.
- Thailand's ambassador in Rabat said victims "usually willingly applied" for Thai visas. That framing shifts blame onto victims [MENA-18].

**Consular reach**
- The case went through the embassy in Bangkok.
- On 17 May 2024 an NGO, The Exodus Road, reported it to the Moroccan embassy, the Myanmar embassy and Thailand's foreign ministry. A month later there was no progress [MENA-17].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment

- **February 2021 takedown.** Meta removed 385 Facebook accounts, 6 Pages and 40 Instagram accounts. They mostly originated in Morocco and targeted domestic audiences. Meta found them after Amnesty International shared information (Meta report, 3 Mar 2021) [MENA-8].
- **May 2022 takedown.** Meta removed 43 more accounts after a DFRLab investigation (DFRLab, 17 Nov 2022) [MENA-9].
  - Targets included the jailed journalists Omar Radi and Soulaimane Raissouni and other critics.
  - The accounts left derogatory and sexualised comments under posts from state-aligned outlets (Chouf TV, Barlamane), used stock-photo profile pictures and posted in coordinated bursts.
  - They also attacked reporting on the Pegasus spyware.
  - DFRLab named no state body or firm, and gave no evidence about who ran the accounts or whether they were paid.

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment

**Cases and numbers**
- **May 2024:** about 14 Moroccans were reportedly held on the Thai–Myanmar border. They had been recruited from Morocco through local intermediaries for fake e-commerce jobs, and routed through Malaysia and Thailand to Mae Sot. The embassy in Bangkok was following the case (Morocco World News, 8 May 2024) [MENA-18].
- **21 Moroccans** sought help (The Thaiger, 1 Jul 2024) [MENA-17].
  - They were promised e-commerce jobs in Thailand at US$1,000, rising to US$2,000.
  - One group left Morocco on 25 Feb, landed at Bangkok's Don Mueang airport on 2 Mar, and was taken by minivan from Mae Sot across the river to a scam base in Karen State.
  - They were beaten and threatened. Ransom demands were US$6,000–7,000 (about 250,000 baht).
- **5 Jul 2024:** 12 Moroccans were brought out through the Tak border checkpoint with the Royal Thai Army and the Moroccan embassy. Seven others had already been freed after ransoms, and 2 chose to stay. This group had been lured with casino jobs (Hespress, 6 Jul 2024) [MENA-16].
- **July 2024:** 18 youths reached Bangkok: 6 paid ransoms and 12 were freed after an NGO intervened. An earlier group of 25 had already returned to Morocco. Recruiters reportedly earned commissions (Al3omk, 15 Jul 2024) [MENA-19].
- **Official count:** 34 identified victims (24 in Myanmar, 5 in Laos, 5 in Cambodia), plus 5 more in Cambodia, for 39 in total. The ministry paid 26 air tickets and some hotel costs (Foreign Minister Nasser Bourita, answering a written question in parliament; Hespress, 28 Sep 2024) [MENA-14].
  - Bourita said social-media claims of 100 or 400 detainees lacked evidence [MENA-14].
  - The ministry did not answer AP's questions about the total (Nov 2025) [MENA-12].

**Prosecutions**
- From about October 2024, an investigating judge at the Casablanca Court of Appeal heard witnesses. Four defendants were in custody; three had been arrested on arrival under national warrants [MENA-15].
- On 4 Nov 2025 a court convicted Nabil Moafik in Morocco's first scam-compound trafficking case: 5 years in prison and US$107,300 in fines (AP via Arab News; Hespress, 5 Nov 2025) [MENA-12][MENA-13]. How the recruitment worked:
  - He posted a Thailand call-centre job in a Facebook group for Moroccans in Türkiye.
  - A recruiter sent money for a plane ticket to Malaysia.
  - Victims were taken to Myanmar and some were freed only after paying ransoms in cryptocurrency.
  - Moafik said he earned US$21–107 per recruit, and denied knowing about the exploitation.
- Moroccans deceptively recruited into the Russian army: not researched (gap).

### 2.5 Israel-based and Tunisia-based firms targeting Africa: Archimedes Group and UReputation

**Archimedes Group (Israel)** (Meta, 16 May 2019) [MENA-4]
- What Meta removed: 65 Facebook accounts, 161 Pages, 23 Groups, 12 events and 4 Instagram accounts.
- Reach: about 2.8 million accounts followed the Pages.
- Ad spend: about US$812,000, paid in Brazilian reais, Israeli shekels and US dollars. Ads ran from December 2012 to April 2019.
- Targets: Nigeria, Senegal, Togo, Angola, Niger and Tunisia, with some activity in Latin America and Southeast Asia.
- Tactics: posing as locals and as local news outlets, and publishing supposedly leaked material about politicians.
- Outcome: Meta banned the company and its subsidiaries and sent a cease-and-desist letter.
- DFRLab's figure of "at least 13 countries" is UNVERIFIED (snippet only; DFRLab Medium page returned 403).

**UReputation (Tunisia)**
- **Meta (5 Jun 2020)** [MENA-1]
  - Removed 182 Facebook accounts, 446 Pages, 96 Groups, 60 events and 209 Instagram accounts.
  - Targeted Francophone countries in Sub-Saharan Africa, using fake accounts posing as locals and Pages posing as independent news outlets.
- **DFRLab, "Operation Carthage" (5 Jun 2020)** [MENA-2][MENA-3]
  - More than 900 assets targeting people in 10 African countries.
  - Supported Togolese President Faure Gnassingbé's February 2020 re-election and Henri Konan Bédié's campaign for Côte d'Ivoire's October 2020 election. It also targeted Tunisia's 2019 presidential race, and an image caption names Comoros' President Azali Assoumani.
  - Reach: about 3.8 million Page followers, about 132,000 Group members and more than 171,000 Instagram followers.
  - Ad spend: about US$331,000, mostly in euros and US dollars.
  - DFRLab called it financially motivated, with no consistent ideology, and a warning about PR firms selling disinformation for profit.
- **Workers:** None of the sources we opened says how UReputation or Archimedes hired the people running the fake accounts, how they were paid, or whether they understood the deception (gap). For job seekers, the takeaway is practical: a "community manager" or "social media" job at an agency that runs many pages and personas pretending to be local people in another country, for political clients, is exactly the work these takedowns targeted.

## 3. Synthesis for job seekers in MENA

### 3.1 What to know

- **"A job in Thailand" can end in Myanmar.** Moroccans offered e-commerce, call-centre or casino jobs in Thailand were driven from Mae Sot across the river into Myanmar and forced to scam people online [MENA-16][MENA-17]. Ask for the employer's full legal name and address, and check that company on Thailand's official business register yourself (Thai registry URL not verified here). Never accept "we'll explain when you arrive".
- **Very high pay for no experience is the bait.** One documented offer was US$1,000 a month at first, rising to US$2,000 [MENA-17]. A paid flight to Malaysia or Bangkok, but no work visa issued before you travel, is a warning sign [MENA-12][MENA-18].
- **The recruiter may be one of your own people, even inside a diaspora group.** The convicted Moroccan recruiter worked through a Facebook group for Moroccans in Türkiye and was paid per person he sent [MENA-12]. Friendly WhatsApp or Facebook "brokers" are not licensed agencies.
- **Check the licence before you pay anything.**
  - Egypt: use the Ministry of Labour's licence-check service and its published list of suspended companies [MENA-35].
  - Jordan: check the Ministry of Labour directory of private employment offices and its list of cancelled licences [MENA-37].
  - Morocco and Tunisia: ask ANAPEC or ATCT directly (their sites could not be checked for this report).
- **If you are trapped, you may be pushed to recruit friends or pay ransom.** Victims were told to pay or bring in 100 more people, and ransoms ran to US$6,000–7,000 [MENA-12][MENA-17]. Recruiting others can expose you to trafficking charges at home [MENA-15][MENA-19]. Tell your embassy everything early.
- **"Page admin" and "social media" jobs can be influence-for-hire.** Firms in Tunisia, Egypt and Israel have run hundreds of fake news Pages and fake local profiles for clients in other countries' elections [MENA-1][MENA-4][MENA-5][MENA-6]. Walk away if the job means running many accounts, pretending to be from another country, or posting political content for an unnamed "client".
- **Know where help is.** Your country may have no embassy in Myanmar, Laos or Cambodia: Morocco's cases went through Bangkok, Tunisia's through Jakarta [MENA-17][MENA-20]. In Egypt, report online crime on 108 and call police on 122 [MENA-33]. For children, call 16000 [MENA-34].

**Labour-mobility schemes are the main legal migration route, and they have documented abuse.** Australia's Pacific Australia Labour Mobility (PALM) scheme:
- **Size:** more than 30,000 workers in Australia at any one time [PAC-3][PAC-4].
- **Shrinking seasonal work:** PALM seasonal work in agriculture fell 24% between July 2023 and July 2024 [PAC-3].
- **Fiji supplies the most workers:** 6,379, up from 266 in 2019. This figure comes from a photo caption citing Department of Home Affairs data [PAC-3].
- **Asylum claims:** PALM workers lodged an average of 244 asylum applications a month in 2023/24 (almost 3,000 a year), up from 53 a month in 2020/21. Professor Stephen Howes of ANU cited Department of Home Affairs data (RNZ, 20 Nov 2024) [PAC-3].
- **Absconding and enforcement** (SBS News, 27 Apr 2025) [PAC-4]:
  - More than 7,000 PALM workers absconded over five years.
  - There were 494 approved employers, 102 of them labour-hire firms.
  - By June 2024 the Fair Work Ombudsman had opened 228 investigations into PALM employers and recovered A$762,625 for 1,937 workers.
  - Home Affairs cancelled 983 offshore visas in 2024 because workers had disengaged from the scheme.
- **Why workers who leave are exposed:** workers are tied to one employer and cannot start a transfer themselves. Leaving can cancel both the visa and the health insurance, which pushes those who leave into cash-in-hand work and new exploitation [PAC-4].
- **Official concern:** a September 2024 paper by the NSW Anti-slavery Commissioner's office, titled *Be Our Guests*, addresses urgent modern-slavery risks for temporary migrant workers in rural and regional New South Wales. We opened the PDF, but its text could not be machine-read, so only the title and file date are cited [PAC-8].

## P3. What a Pacific job seeker should know

- **Use your government's labour-mobility unit and an approved employer.** Real PALM and seasonal jobs come that way, not from a Facebook "agent" who wants a fee. The official PALM site (palmscheme.gov.au) timed out during this research, so check it yourself.
- **Your visa is tied to your employer.** If you leave, you can lose your visa and health cover. "Better cash jobs" offered to people who have left are a known route into exploitation [PAC-4]. Report problems to the Fair Work Ombudsman or the scheme's welfare channels; the Ombudsman has recovered wages for almost 2,000 PALM workers [PAC-4].
- **Be wary of anyone selling a "protection visa" or "visa change" for a fee.** Thousands of PALM workers have applied for asylum [PAC-3]. That a paid "fixer" will make your claim succeed is UNVERIFIED and likely false.
- **Treat Asia jobs with free flights as a trap until proven safe.** Offers of customer-service, data-entry or "online marketing" jobs in Thailand, Cambodia, Myanmar, Laos or Dubai, with free flights and big salaries, follow the scam-compound pattern documented in other regions (see the cross-cutting and other regional sections). No Pacific-specific case was verified here.
- **Election-season page jobs may be influence work.** With Fiji's election and referendum coming [PAC-7], paid offers to run political Facebook pages or several profiles for an unnamed "client" may be influence-for-hire. Ask who the client is, and refuse to post under fake names.
