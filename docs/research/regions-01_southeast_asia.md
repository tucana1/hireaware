# Southeast Asia (excluding the Philippines)

> Scope: Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Myanmar, Cambodia, Laos. Research date 2026-11-07. Citation prefix [SEA-n]; full source list in section 5. Lines marked **Analysis** are the researcher's interpretation of the cited facts, not facts in their own right. Lines marked **UNVERIFIED** were not confirmed on a page that was opened (usually a search snippet only, or the official site would not load).
>
> Research note: the shared web-search budget ran out part-way through this section, so some planned checks (payment-app statistics, Myanmar/Lao regulator detail, Laos 2025–26 raids, Cambodia/Laos disinformation cases) could not be done. These are listed in section 4.

## 1. Regional picture: what shapes the risk here

- **Host vs origin.** Cambodia, Myanmar's armed-group-held border zones and Laos's Golden Triangle SEZ have hosted the compounds. Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia are mainly *origin* countries, and Thailand (Mae Sot, Bangkok) is also the main *transit and rescue* corridor. Indonesians are the largest documented Southeast Asian origin group in this research: 12,019 Indonesians asked the embassy in Phnom Penh for help getting home in January–June 2026 alone (KBRI Phnom Penh via Kompas, 4 Jul 2026) [SEA-18].
- **The crackdown moved the problem rather than ending it.** Cambodia's anti-scam commission says large compounds were "entirely eradicated" between July 2025 and August 2026. The same official acknowledged that groups have shifted to smaller, decentralised operations (Xinhua via The Star, 28 Aug 2026) [SEA-56]. Across the border, more than 5,300 people were still estimated to be trapped in four compounds in DKBA-controlled areas of Myanmar in June 2026 (CSNHTV via Reuters, 23 Jun 2026) [SEA-41].
- **The line between victim and offender is thin, and governments are prosecuting returnees more often.** Vietnam charged 346 people deported from Cambodia, 343 of them detained (Viet Nam News, 21 Apr 2026) [SEA-30]. Indonesia's embassy says it is working with Indonesian police to check how deeply each returnee was involved (ANTARA, 27 Mar 2026) [SEA-17]. Thailand said people who joined voluntarily would face legal action (The Nation, 23 Feb 2025) [SEA-39]. Cambodia bars deportees from re-entering the country permanently (Xinhua, 16 Jul 2026) [SEA-58]. Officials in Indonesia and Vietnam openly say some workers went knowingly for the high pay [SEA-13][SEA-15][SEA-30]. **Analysis:** a job seeker who is deceived can still end up with a criminal record, so warnings should say this plainly.
- **How people are recruited is very similar across the region.** Recruiters post in Facebook and Telegram job groups. Common job titles are customer service, admin, "computer operator" and e-commerce operator. A sponsor arranges the passport, visa and ticket, and workers travel on tourist visas through Singapore, Malaysia or Bangkok [SEA-19][SEA-20]. More and more, recruitment runs through friends and family: workers are told to bring in a replacement before they can leave [SEA-15], or are paid a bonus for each new recruit [SEA-30].
- **Disinformation work comes in two models.** In Vietnam, Myanmar and Thailand, influence operations are run by the state or military and staffed by soldiers or officials acting on orders [SEA-26][SEA-27][SEA-36][SEA-51]. Indonesia and Malaysia have a commercial, party-linked "buzzer" or "cybertrooper" gig market. There, students and young graduates are paid per post, per project or per month, often through marketing agencies or WhatsApp/Telegram groups [SEA-22][SEA-23][SEA-44][SEA-45]. **Analysis:** the risk of being *unknowingly* recruited into disinformation work is highest in Indonesia and Malaysia, where it looks like ordinary social-media gig work.
- **Economic pressure differs by country.** Malaysia's household debt is high (84.8% of GDP) [SEA-5], and so is Thailand's (86.7% of GDP) [SEA-4]. About 58% of Indonesia's workers are informal [SEA-3]. Youth unemployment is about 12–13% in Indonesia and Malaysia [SEA-1]. Vietnamese labour migrants pay about US$4,830 each to go abroad, and 53% borrow to do it [SEA-34]. About 900,000 Cambodian workers came home from Thailand during the 2025 border conflict [SEA-60]. Myanmar's 2024 conscription law pushes young people across the border without papers [SEA-52].
- **Platforms differ by country, and recruiters use Telegram everywhere.** Zalo is dominant in Vietnam, with 78.3m monthly users [SEA-7], and LINE in Thailand, with 56m [SEA-8]. Facebook is the default in Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar [SEA-10][SEA-11][SEA-12]. TikTok reaches most adults in every country listed [SEA-6]–[SEA-11]. Scam recruiters, the Myanmar military's online units and Malaysian cybertrooper coordinators all use Telegram or WhatsApp groups to hand out tasks [SEA-19][SEA-45][SEA-51].

## 2. Country profiles

### 2.1 Indonesia

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money**
  - Youth unemployment (15–24, modelled ILO estimate): 12.98% in 2025 (World Bank, data updated 8 Oct 2026) [SEA-1].
  - Informal workers: 57.80% of employed people, about 84.58m, in August 2025 (BPS via CNBC Indonesia, 5 Nov 2025) [SEA-3].
  - Remittances are not a big share of the national economy: 1.22% of GDP in 2025 (World Bank) [SEA-2]. **Analysis:** the pressure comes from household-level dependence in sending districts more than from national dependence; this research did not measure it.
- **Migration & agencies**
  - Placement is regulated by KP2MI, formerly BP2MI (see d).
  - Online-scam recruitment mostly goes around the formal system. Recruiters use social-media job groups and "sponsors" who arrange passports, visas and tickets, and recruits travel on tourist visas. Routes reported by police: Medan–Batam–Singapore–Cambodia, Jakarta–Singapore–Cambodia and Batam–Malaysia–Cambodia (Bareskrim via Jawa Pos, 9 Feb 2026) [SEA-19].
  - Recruiters coach people to pass airport checks by travelling in small groups as tourists and giving false reasons for the trip (Kemlu via Kompas, 24 Apr 2025) [SEA-15].
- **Who is targeted**
  - Recruits include both men and women. The March 2025 Myawaddy evacuation group was 449 men and 105 women (CNBC Indonesia, 18 Mar 2025) [SEA-16].
  - Trafficking-victim records for 2021–2025 in all sectors list 1,276 adult women, 44 adult men, 1,096 girls and 137 boys (SIMFONI PPA via CNBC Indonesia, 31 Jul 2026) [SEA-21]. This dataset covers all trafficking, not only scam centres.
- **Platforms & payments**
  - Internet users: 230m, 80.5% of the population (DataReportal, 5 Nov 2025) [SEA-6].
  - Ad reach: Facebook 121m, Instagram 108m, TikTok 180m adults [SEA-6].
  - WhatsApp is not covered in that report, and no WhatsApp figure was verified.
  - Recruitment ads appear in Facebook and Telegram job groups [SEA-19].
  - Payment apps (DANA, OVO, GoPay): UNVERIFIED, not sourced in this pass.
  - Pay inside compounds was reported as Rp6–8m, sometimes in irregular cash or not paid at all [SEA-19].
- **Politics & elections 2026–27**
  - No national election in 2026–27. The next general election is expected in 2029 (UNVERIFIED; not checked against KPU).
  - Buzzers were active in the 2024 presidential race, and the election supervisory agency Bawaslu announced measures against them (ABC, 20 Jan 2024) [SEA-22].
- **Disinfo industry model:** mainly commercial and party- or campaign-linked. Marketing agencies broker deals between politicians and buzzers [SEA-22]. The government has also paid influencers to promote policies [SEA-24]. Details in (b).
- **Legal risks to victims**
  - Kemlu says not everyone recorded is a trafficking victim, and some went voluntarily for the pay (IDX Channel, 21 Oct 2025) [SEA-13].
  - The embassy in Phnom Penh is working with Indonesian police to check each returnee's level of involvement (ANTARA, 27 Mar 2026) [SEA-17].
  - People found still doing scam work in Cambodia were warned to expect legal consequences (Kompas, 4 Jul 2026) [SEA-18].
  - Many returnees also overstayed visas: Cambodia waived overstay fines for 5,950 Indonesians [SEA-18].
- **Consular reach**
  - Myanmar is harder to work in than Cambodia because parts of it are held by armed non-state groups (Kemlu PWNI Director Judha Nugraha, Kompas, 24 Apr 2025) [SEA-15].
  - Getting the March 2025 Myawaddy group out took about 10 hours on 13 buses to Thailand, then a 6-hour drive to Bangkok, because Mae Sot had too few flights [SEA-16].
  - In Cambodia, the embassy issued 2,346 emergency travel documents (SPLP) to people with no passport and ran a shelter for up to 300 people [SEA-17].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **Per-post pay rates for "buzzers"** (Tempo English, 6 Dec 2019) [SEA-23]:
  - Small accounts ("micro-buzzers"): Rp100,000–200,000 per post.
  - Large accounts ("macro-buzzers"): Rp700,000–2,000,000 per post on Twitter or Facebook.
  - Same report: President Jokowi's 2019 campaign had a central content team of about 735 people that sent material by WhatsApp to regional volunteers, who posted it from their own accounts. The campaign coordinator said these volunteers were not paid per post.
- **The industry in the 2024 election cycle** (ABC News, 20 Jan 2024) [SEA-22]:
  - A long-time buzzer said he earned about US$10,000 a month on top of his job, and that a group linked to a candidate offered him more than Rp100m a month in 2019.
  - Getting a topic onto the morning trending list cost about Rp70m.
  - Junior "cyber troopers" earned Rp1m–50m per project, depending on reach (figure from a 2019 Reuters report, cited by ABC).
  - Full influencer packages could reach Rp1bn.
  - Marketing agencies often act as middlemen. Many juniors are young graduates doing basic posting.
  - Teams use fake personas, such as an account posing as a housewife. The 2019 "Janda" account, actually run by a middle-aged man, reportedly reached at least a million people a week.
  - **Did workers know?** Senior operators clearly did; the buzzer interviewed called it pure business. The article does not say whether junior posters understood they were spreading propaganda.
- **Government-paid influencers** (The Jakarta Post, 8 Sep 2020) [SEA-24]:
  - Influencers pushed the hashtag #IndonesiaButuhKerja in support of the job-creation bill.
  - Several, including radio host Gofar Hilman and musician Ardhito Pramono, later apologised for endorsing it.
  - **Analysis:** this is an example of paid promoters who may not have fully understood the political campaign they were part of.

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- **Scale**
  - Indonesians recorded in online-scam cases since 2020: more than 10,000, across 10 countries (Kemlu Director of Citizen Protection Judha Nugraha, IDX Channel, 21 Oct 2025) [SEA-13].
  - Trafficking (TPPO) online-scam cases recorded by Kemlu: 7,027, in 10 countries (seven in Southeast Asia; named non-Southeast Asian destinations include the UAE, South Africa and Belarus) (Kompas, 24 Apr 2025) [SEA-14].
  - UNODC/Jobstreet figures: more than 3,200 Indonesians recorded in Southeast Asian online-scam cases, of whom more than 1,100 were identified as trafficking victims (CNBC Indonesia, 31 Jul 2026) [SEA-21].
- **Myanmar repatriations in 2025**
  - Kemlu figures: 699 Indonesians in three waves, 46 on 20 Feb, 84 on 28 Feb and 569 on 18–19 Mar 2025 (Kompas, 24 Apr 2025) [SEA-14].
  - CNBC Indonesia reported the March wave as 554 people (449 men, 105 women) (18 Mar 2025) [SEA-16]. The two sources disagree on the size of the March wave.
- **Cambodia in 2026**
  - 16 Jan–26 Mar 2026: 6,308 Indonesians who had left scam syndicates asked the embassy for help. 2,528 were repatriated, and Cambodia waived overstay fines for 4,361 (KBRI Phnom Penh via ANTARA, 27 Mar 2026) [SEA-17].
  - By 30 June 2026: 12,019 had asked for repatriation help and 5,487 returns had been arranged. Many had no passport, and some Indonesians were still arriving to join networks (Kompas, 4 Jul 2026) [SEA-18].
- **Lures documented**
  - Early offers (2021–22): customer-service or marketing jobs paying US$1,000–1,200 a month (Kemlu via Kompas, 24 Apr 2025) [SEA-15].
  - Big performance bonuses: one worker described a bonus of Rp150m a month for defrauding up to Rp3bn [SEA-15].
  - "Tukar kepala" (head swap): people who want to go home must first recruit friends or relatives to replace them [SEA-15].
  - Job titles in Facebook/Telegram groups: e-commerce operator, customer service, service staff, online-gambling operator. Reality: 14–18-hour days with targets and no permission to leave (Bareskrim, Jawa Pos, 9 Feb 2026) [SEA-19].
  - A December 2025 case (9 victims): a sponsor promised "computer operator" work at Rp9m a month, arranged passports, visas and tickets, then took the passports. Workers who missed targets were punished with exercise drills such as 300 laps of a futsal field (Bareskrim, ANTARA, 27 Dec 2025) [SEA-20].
- **Trend:** IOM forced-criminality cases linked to online scams in Southeast Asia: 296 in 2022, 978 in 2023 and 492 in 2024 (CNBC Indonesia, 31 Jul 2026) [SEA-21].

### 2.2 Vietnam

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money**
  - Youth unemployment: 6.17% in 2025 (World Bank) [SEA-1].
  - The World Bank series has no remittances-to-GDP figure for Vietnam for 2022–2025 [SEA-2].
- **Migration & agencies**
  - Workers sent abroad in 2025: 144,345, 11% above plan. Main destinations: Japan (more than 64,600), Taiwan (more than 57,000), South Korea (more than 11,600) (Báo Chính phủ, 8 Jan 2026) [SEA-33].
  - Average cost to go abroad in 2025: US$4,830 (VND125.7m). About 53% of workers borrowed, an average of VND122m. Payments to recruitment companies and brokers were the largest single cost, about VND53m (~US$2,040). Officials blamed informal brokers for extra charges (NSO/IOM survey via Tuoi Tre, 8 Sep 2026) [SEA-34].
  - **Analysis:** debt-financed migration is normal in Vietnam, so a "free ticket" scam offer looks attractive by comparison.
- **Who is targeted**
  - Women as well as men: more than 120 of the 346 people charged after a Cambodian scam deportation were women (Viet Nam News, 21 Apr 2026) [SEA-30].
  - Minors too: a recruiter used several Facebook accounts to recruit four people, including children aged 13 and 15 (Viet Nam News, 1 Jul 2025) [SEA-31].
- **Platforms & payments**
  - Zalo: 78.3m monthly active users (91.4% of internet users). Facebook ad reach: 79.0m. TikTok ad reach: 76.1m adults (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [SEA-7].
  - Recruiter payments in one trafficking case went by bank transfer and cash [SEA-31].
  - MoMo usage: UNVERIFIED, not sourced in this pass.
- **Politics & elections 2026–27**
  - The 16th National Assembly election was held in March 2026: official turnout 99.7%, more than 76m voters, 500 deputies (Viet Nam News, 6 Apr 2026) [SEA-35].
  - It was a single-party, non-competitive election. No further national vote is due before 2031.
- **Disinfo industry model:** state- and military-run (Force 47, see b).
- **Legal risks to victims**
  - Vietnam is prosecuting people who come back from scam compounds.
  - Dong Nai provincial police had detained 343 suspects by 27 Mar 2026. A further 331 returnees were fined for illegal entry or having no valid passport (VietNamNet, 30 Mar 2026) [SEA-29].
  - The Ministry of Public Security charged 346 people deported from Cambodia on 17 Mar 2026 (343 detained) with using computer and telecom networks to take property (Viet Nam News, 21 Apr 2026) [SEA-30].
  - Of a group of 59 returnees in January 2026, 23 were fined for crossing the border without completing exit procedures (Viet Nam News, 30 Jan 2026) [SEA-28].
  - The foreign ministry says citizens who broke the law abroad will be dealt with, and that victims will get consular protection (MOFA spokesperson Pham Thu Hang, VOV, 27 Feb 2025) [SEA-32].
- **Consular reach**
  - After the February 2025 Myawaddy crackdown, the ministry was "still verifying" how many Vietnamese were held and told its missions in Thailand and Myanmar to identify them [SEA-32].
  - Returnees from Cambodia mostly come back through border-gate handovers, such as Tân Nam in January 2026 [SEA-28].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **Force 47.** In December 2017, Vietnam announced a 10,000-strong military cyber unit to fight "wrong views". It was announced by Lt. Gen. Nguyen Trong Nghia, deputy head of the army's political department (Reuters via The World, 26 Dec 2017) [SEA-26].
- **How it is staffed** (Reuters Institute, 10 Aug 2021) [SEA-27]:
  - Staff are military officers trained to treat shaping opinion as part of the job.
  - A scholar quoted said this makes the unit cheap, because officers are unlikely to ask for extra pay.
  - A researcher quoted described minimal command and control over individual members.
- **Recruitment and pay:** recruitment is by military assignment, not job ads, and there is no evidence of per-post payment [SEA-27].
- **Analysis:** the risk that civilian job seekers are hired *unknowingly* into Vietnam's state influence work appears low. Vietnamese job seekers' main exposure is to scam-compound recruitment.
- Other pro-government commentator programmes (e.g., "dư luận viên") were not verified in this pass.

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- **Lures:** social-media ads promising "easy work, high pay" ("việc nhẹ lương cao") of VND15–25m (~US$570–950) a month. Most of the 346 people charged went to Cambodia voluntarily for salaries and commissions (Viet Nam News, 21 Apr 2026) [SEA-30][SEA-29].
- **Reported monthly pay inside the compound** [SEA-30]:
  - "Sales" (fake personas): about US$200 base plus 8–15% commission.
  - Customer service: about US$800 plus commission.
  - Telephone staff: US$200–500.
  - Translators: about US$600.
  - Money-laundering "banking" team: VND40–75m.
  - HR recruiters: US$300 base plus US$200 for each new Vietnamese recruit.
  - **Analysis:** that bonus is an incentive to recruit friends and family.
- **Recruitment of minors** (Huế court, Viet Nam News, 1 Jul 2025) [SEA-31]:
  - A 17-year-old recruiter, herself lured to Sihanoukville, used Facebook to recruit four people, including children aged 13 and 15. She was paid VND30m.
  - A 13-year-old who tried to escape was charged US$700 plus VND8.32m to be smuggled home.
  - Sentences ranged from 15 months to 13 years 3 months.
- **Handovers from Cambodia:** 59 citizens came back on 30 Jan 2026 (42 men, 17 women, from 23 provinces), some from scam compounds doing romance, gambling-promotion and investment scams. 27 had left Vietnam illegally [SEA-28].
- **Myanmar:** a reported 685 Vietnamese were among about 8,000 people freed in 2025, with about 215 still stuck at the Thai border in May 2025. UNVERIFIED (snippet only; UCA News page blocked).

### 2.3 Thailand

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money**
  - Youth unemployment: 4.53% in 2025 (World Bank) [SEA-1].
  - Household debt: 86.7% of GDP in Q4 2025, 16.44 trillion baht. As bank credit gets harder to obtain, households are turning to non-bank lenders such as cooperatives and pawnshops (Bank of Thailand data via The Nation/The Star, 3 Apr 2026) [SEA-4].
  - Remittances: 2.06% of GDP in 2025 [SEA-2].
- **Migration & agencies**
  - Only agencies licensed by the Department of Employment may legally send Thai workers abroad (UNVERIFIED: news and search snippets; the DOE site timed out).
  - Fake farm-work offers in Australia and New Zealand are a common scam aimed at Thai job seekers (UNVERIFIED: snippets).
- **Who is targeted:** women as well as men. Of 109 Thais rescued in Poipet in February 2025, 54 were women (The Nation via The Star, 23 Feb 2025) [SEA-39].
- **Platforms & payments**
  - LINE: 56m monthly active users (78.2% of the population). Facebook ad reach 51.5m; TikTok ad reach 56.6m adults (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [SEA-8].
  - PromptPay and TrueMoney: UNVERIFIED, not sourced in this pass.
- **Politics & elections 2026–27**
  - Early general election on 8 Feb 2026. Certified results: Bhumjaithai 191, People's Party 120, Pheu Thai 74, with one seat pending (AFP via The Star, 6 Mar 2026) [SEA-42].
  - Anutin Charnvirakul was re-elected prime minister on 19 Mar 2026, leading a coalition with Pheu Thai (Wikipedia, accessed 7 Nov 2026) [SEA-43].
- **Disinfo industry model:** state and military information operations (IO) by the Royal Thai Army and the Internal Security Operations Command (ISOC), see (b).
- **Legal risks to victims**
  - After the February 2025 Poipet raid, officials said people who had joined voluntarily would face legal action, and people who had been deceived would be allowed home [SEA-39].
  - In October 2025, all 16 Thais rescued from Shwe Kokko were put through the National Referral Mechanism, Thailand's screening for possible trafficking victims (The Nation via The Star, 6 Oct 2025) [SEA-40].
- **Consular reach:** Thailand runs the main border checkpoints (Mae Sot–Myawaddy; Ban Klong Luek–Poipet) and handles screening for other countries' nationals as well [SEA-39][SEA-40][SEA-47].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **Twitter takedown, October 2020.** On 8 Oct 2020, Twitter removed 926 accounts it attributed to the Royal Thai Army, out of 1,594 accounts removed across five countries (Prachatai English, 12 Oct 2020) [SEA-37].
- **Stanford Internet Observatory analysis** (8 Oct 2020) [SEA-36]:
  - Only 455 of the accounts ever tweeted; together they posted 21,385 tweets.
  - Most accounts were created around January 2020 and were most active in February 2020.
  - They praised the army and attacked the Future Forward and Move Forward parties.
  - SIO called it "a coordinated but low-impact operation".
- **Allegations in parliament.** In a no-confidence debate on 25 Feb 2020, MP Viroj Lakkana-adisorn alleged that the military paid for IO and cyber-attacks out of state budgets. He showed an ISOC document funding a website for the deep south [SEA-37].
- **Army response:** the army denied running the accounts (BenarNews, 9 Oct 2020) [SEA-38].
- **Who did the work:** none of these sources says how the operators were recruited or paid. **Analysis:** the evidence points to work inside state institutions, not to hiring members of the public.

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment
- **Poipet, Cambodia, 23 Feb 2025:** a joint Thai–Cambodian raid freed 215 people, including 109 Thais (54 women), 50 Pakistanis, 48 Indians and 3 Indonesians. Cambodian officials questioned everyone to separate gang members from victims [SEA-39].
- **Shwe Kokko/Myawaddy, from 5 Oct 2025:** 16 Thais were repatriated and screened through the National Referral Mechanism [SEA-40].
- **Still trapped, June 2026:** about 20 Thais were estimated to be among the more than 5,300 people held in DKBA-area compounds (CSNHTV via Reuters, 23 Jun 2026) [SEA-41].
- **Lures used on Thai recruits:** not described in the opened sources (gap).

### 2.4 Malaysia

#### a) Vulnerability factors
- **Jobs & money**
  - Youth unemployment: 11.95% in 2025 (World Bank) [SEA-1].
  - Household debt: 84.8% of GDP. The median debt-service ratio is 41% of income on new loans (BNM Financial Stability Review 1H25 via The Star, 16 Oct 2025) [SEA-5].
  - Remittances: 1.89% of GDP in 2025 [SEA-2].
- **Migration & agencies:** recruitment agencies are licensed under the Private Employment Agencies Act 1981 (Act 246) by the Labour Department (JTKSM) (see d).
- **Who is targeted:** students and young adults are drawn into paid cybertrooper work. Victims of overseas job scams include adults of both sexes (see b and c).
- **Platforms & payments**
  - Facebook ad reach 23.0m; TikTok ad reach 30.7m adults; Instagram 16.1m (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [SEA-9].
  - Cybertroopers receive instructions in WhatsApp and Telegram groups and are paid into bank accounts [SEA-45].
  - Touch 'n Go: UNVERIFIED, not sourced in this pass.
- **Politics & elections 2026–27**
  - Sabah state election, 29 Nov 2025: hung assembly, GRS largest with 29 seats, Warisan 25, BN 6, turnout 64.35% (The Star, 30 Nov 2025) [SEA-48].
  - The Sarawak state assembly was dissolved on 26 Oct 2026, so a 13th state election is due shortly. It has 82 seats (The Vibes, 23 Oct 2026) [SEA-49].
  - The general election (GE16) must be held by about February 2028 (UNVERIFIED: snippet, latest polling date 17 Feb 2028). There is speculation about an earlier vote.
- **Disinfo industry model:** party-linked but increasingly commercial and freelance. Until 2018 the governing parties funded most cybertroopers; after the change of government, many became freelancers working for whichever party pays (ISEAS Fulcrum, 18 Aug 2023) [SEA-44].
- **Legal risks to victims:** victims repatriated in December 2025 were handed to the Royal Malaysia Police at the Bukit Kayu Hitam border complex [SEA-47]. Whether victims are prosecuted: no information found (gap).
- **Consular reach**
  - The December 2025 repatriation involved the embassies in Bangkok and Yangon, the consulate-general in Songkhla, and Thai Tak immigration under Thailand's National Referral Mechanism [SEA-47].
  - People rescued in Cambodia in February 2025 were first held at an immigration detention centre in Siem Reap (Bernama via The Star, 26 Feb 2025) [SEA-46].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment
- **Academic evidence** (ISEAS Fulcrum, Loh & Ali, 18 Aug 2023) [SEA-44]:
  - The authors describe cybertroopers as anonymous, politically linked actors who are often paid.
  - They found at least 5,000 mentions of the insult "cytro" on Twitter in the month before the 12 Aug 2023 state elections.
  - Cybertroopers aligned with the Perikatan Nasional coalition dominated Facebook; those aligned with Pakatan Harapan and Barisan Nasional were more aggressive on Twitter.
  - The authors could not tell paid accounts apart from genuine supporters.
- **Industry exposé** (The Vibes, 24 May 2026, citing Kosmo! Ahad and anonymous sources; claims not independently verified) [SEA-45]:
  - Pay: ordinary cybertroopers about RM2,000 a month, paid into bank accounts. State coordinators RM5,000–10,000 a month. Non-political engagement tasks RM50–200 each.
  - Organisation: coordinators, state leaders and grassroots operatives. Instructions come through WhatsApp and Telegram groups; one group had about 30 members. Recruits get ready-made fake accounts.
  - Recruits: the work is described as attractive to students.
  - **Analysis:** students are recruited for paid "engagement" work and handed pre-built fake accounts, which is the classic route into unknowingly working for a troll operation.

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment

## 3. Synthesis for job seekers in Southeast Asia

### 3.1 What to know
- **"Customer service", "admin", "computer operator" or "e-commerce" jobs in Cambodia, Myanmar, Laos or "Thailand" that pay far above local wages and need no experience are the most common scam-compound lure.** Real recruits were promised Rp9m a month or US$1,000–1,200 [SEA-15][SEA-20], and Vietnamese recruits VND15–25m [SEA-30].
- **If someone else is paying your ticket and visa, and tells you to travel as a tourist or say you are on holiday, that is a warning sign, not a perk.** That is exactly how Indonesian recruits were sent through Batam, Singapore and Malaysia [SEA-15][SEA-19]. Legal overseas jobs go through licensed agencies and official portals: SISKOP2MI in Indonesia [SEA-25], DOLAB in Vietnam, the Department of Employment in Thailand, JTKSM in Malaysia, and the labour ministries of Myanmar and Cambodia [SEA-55][SEA-62].
- **Be careful when a friend or relative abroad pushes you to join their job.** Compounds make workers recruit a replacement before they can leave ("tukar kepala") [SEA-15], and pay recruiters about US$200 for each new person [SEA-30].
- **Going home is not the end of the risk.** Returnees have been detained and charged (343 in one Vietnamese province) [SEA-29][SEA-30], examined by police (Indonesia) [SEA-17], and banned from re-entering Cambodia [SEA-58]. If you were tricked, contact your embassy or official hotline early and keep evidence of the original job offer.
- **Your passport is your lifeline.** In Cambodia, many Indonesians who reached their embassy had no passport and needed emergency travel documents [SEA-17][SEA-18]. Never hand your passport to a "sponsor" [SEA-20].
- **Paid "posting", "commenting" or "engagement" gigs can be political influence work.** In Indonesia and Malaysia, people are paid per post (Rp100,000–2m), per assignment (RM50–200) or monthly (about RM2,000) [SEA-23][SEA-45]. They often get ready-made fake accounts and daily scripts through WhatsApp or Telegram groups [SEA-45]. If you are told *what* to post about politicians, and given an account that isn't yours, you are working for a cyber-troop or buzzer operation.
- **Myanmar youth avoiding conscription in Thailand without papers are prime targets for off-the-books "jobs".** Smugglers and brokers are already part of their journey [SEA-52].

  - "operator e-commerce", "admin judi online" [SEA-19]
  - "gaji tinggi dan bonus" (high salary and bonuses) [SEA-15]
  - sponsor "mengurus paspor, visa, tiket" (arranges passport, visa, ticket) [SEA-20]
  - "visa turis" (tourist visa) [SEA-19]
  - "tukar kepala" ("head swap": a replacement recruit) [SEA-15]
- **Indonesian (buzzer work)**

  - "cyber troopers", paid "per proyek" [SEA-22]

- **Bahasa Indonesia (scam compound):** "Dibutuhkan segera CS/operator komputer di Kamboja, gaji Rp9–15 juta + bonus, paspor, visa & tiket diurus sponsor, berangkat pakai visa turis."
