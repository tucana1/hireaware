## 1. Regional picture: what shapes the risk here

- **Kenya is the regional hub for both risks, because it has the biggest connected, English-speaking pool.** Internet use reached 40.5% of the population at the end of 2025, compared with 22.0% in Uganda and 21.7% in Ethiopia (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [AFE-46][AFE-47][AFE-48]. Kenya also has a mature mobile-money system, with 95.31 million registered mobile-money accounts in August 2026 (Central Bank of Kenya) [AFE-51]. This makes it easy to pay "per post" influencers and to collect recruitment fees. Scam-compound recruiters prize English fluency, and one Kenyan victim says he was promised a "customer service" job that needed good English and typing (Citizen Digital, 27 Feb 2025) [AFE-11].
- **The disinformation-for-hire model is different in each country.**
  - **Kenya:** mainly a commercial market. Political clients reach paid influencers through WhatsApp groups and pay them by M-Pesa (Mozilla, 2 Sep 2021) [AFE-1]. In 2026 the state was also drafting a formal paid-influencer budget (Business Daily, 18 Jan 2026) [AFE-4].
  - **Uganda:** the model is state-centred. The 2021 Facebook takedown tied a network to the Government Citizens Interaction Centre, a unit in the ICT Ministry [AFE-6]. Researchers say the 2026 campaign used paid influencers and "buzzer" fake accounts on both sides (ISS, 4 Nov 2026) [AFE-7].
  - **Ethiopia:** the documented network was linked to the national security agency INSA (Meta, 16 Jun 2021) [AFE-9].
- **All three countries supply people to scam compounds on the Thai–Myanmar border, but the scale and the state response differ.**
  - **Ethiopia** reports the largest repatriations: 1,300 nationals brought back from Myanmar, according to the foreign minister's report to parliament (The EastAfrican, ~1 Jan 2026) [AFE-33].
  - **Kenya** says at least 357 Kenyans escaped compounds between October 2025 and January 2026 (Daily Nation, 22 Mar 2026) [AFE-14].
  - **Uganda** has brought people home in small groups of 6 to 26 (Uganda MoFA, Apr–May 2025) [AFE-23][AFE-24].
- **Consular reach is thin.**
  - Uganda covers both Thailand and Myanmar from its High Commission in Kuala Lumpur [AFE-23].
  - Ethiopia ran its Myanmar repatriations through its embassies in India and Japan (ENA, 10 Mar 2025) [AFE-30].
  - Kenya has an embassy in Bangkok and has given consular help to Burundians and Ugandans whose countries have no mission in Thailand (The EastAfrican, 24 Dec 2025) [AFE-13].
- **East Africa is a major source of men deceptively recruited into the Russian army.**
  - A Kenyan intelligence report to MPs put the number of Kenyans recruited at more than 1,000 (Al Jazeera, 16 Mar 2026) [AFE-17].
  - A Ugandan embassy letter counts 27 Ugandans killed or presumed dead (Reuters via Daily Sabah, 30 Oct 2026) [AFE-21].
  - The lures are civilian jobs such as security guard, driver, cook, supermarket worker, or "work in Israel" [AFE-20][AFE-21].
  - Uganda is also a documented source of young women recruited to Russia's Alabuga drone-production site through "work and study" offers (US TIP Report 2025: Uganda) [AFE-29].
- **Governments increasingly treat returnees as complicit, which raises the legal risk for genuine victims.**
  - Kenya told families to buy their own return tickets. It said most of those stuck had entered Thailand on tourist visas marked "employment prohibited" (Kenyans.co.ke, 22 Dec 2025) [AFE-12].
  - 39 Kenyans are serving one-year sentences in Myanmar under its immigration law [AFE-14].
  - Myanmar's state-aligned press described 90 deported Ethiopians as "linked to online fraud" (Eleven Myanmar, 15 May 2025) [AFE-31].
- **Heavy churn among licensed recruiters creates cover for unlicensed brokers.**
  - Kenya struck more than 700 agents off its register in about a year (Citizen Digital, 7 May 2024) [AFE-53].
  - Kenya later deregistered 600 "rogue" agencies (Citizen Digital, 1 Apr 2026) [AFE-18].
  - Uganda revoked 275 labour-export licences (Pulse Uganda, 20 Apr 2026) [AFE-60].
  - Ethiopia replaced its overseas-employment law with Proclamation No. 1389/2025 (ILO, 31 Jul 2026) [AFE-67].
- **Election calendar.**
  - Uganda (January 2026) and Ethiopia (June 2026) have already voted [AFE-36][AFE-38].
  - Kenya's general election is on 10 August 2027, with campaigning from 29 May to 7 August 2027 (The Star, 24 Jun 2026) [AFE-40].
  - A 2026 Konrad-Adenauer-Stiftung study calls "disinformation-for-hire" a major threat to Kenya's 2027 polls (The Star, 21 Jun 2026) [AFE-3].

## 2. Country profiles

### 2.1 Kenya

#### a) Vulnerability factors

- **Jobs & money**
  - Youth unemployment (ages 15–24, modelled ILO estimate): 15.2% in 2025 (World Bank WDI, updated 8 Oct 2026) [AFE-43].
  - Youth not in education, employment or training (NEET): 18.5% in 2021 (World Bank WDI) [AFE-44].
  - Vulnerable employment (own-account plus contributing family workers), a proxy for informality: 63.8% of total employment in 2025 (World Bank WDI, modelled ILO) [AFE-45].
  - Personal remittances: 3.76% of GDP in 2025, about US$5.11 billion (World Bank WDI) [AFE-41][AFE-42].
  - Diaspora inflows in September 2026: US$438.5 million. Of this, US$215.5 million came from North America, US$97.7 million from Europe and US$125.3 million from the rest of the world (Central Bank of Kenya) [AFE-52].
  - Household debt figures: not researched (gap).
- **Migration & agencies**
  - The government actively promotes jobs abroad (for example the "Kazi Majuu" job fairs).
  - At the start of the year before May 2024 the agency register had about 1,200 agents. More than 700 were deregistered within a year (PS Roseline Njogu, Citizen Digital, 7 May 2024) [AFE-53].
  - In January 2026, 390 alleged labour or travel-agency fraud cases were under investigation. Of these, 116 involved travel agents registered with neither the National Employment Authority (NEA) nor the Business Registration Service, and 15 case files went to the Director of Public Prosecutions (Labour CS Alfred Mutua, via Kenyans.co.ke, 14 Sep 2026) [AFE-54].
  - Recruiters for the Russian war used unregistered agencies and individuals in Kenya, Russia, Qatar and the UAE, and moved recruits on tourist visas (Mudavadi to the National Assembly, Citizen Digital, 1 Apr 2026) [AFE-18].
  - Some Kenyans already working in the Middle East quit their jobs to sign up [AFE-18].
- **Who is targeted**
  - Scam compounds: young English speakers with typing skills are recruited for "customer service in Thailand" [AFE-11].
  - Russia: Kenyan officials say the main targets are former security personnel and unemployed youth [AFE-18].
  - Alabuga: young women. A Kenyan woman appeared in an Alabuga Start video saying she came to "work and study" there (VOA, 18 Dec 2024) [AFE-35].
  - Former victims re-recruit: the government says some people rescued earlier went back to the compounds and recruited friends, relatives and neighbours (Daily Nation, 22 Mar 2026) [AFE-14].
- **Platforms & payments**
  - Advertising audiences in late 2025: TikTok 18.4 million (adults), Facebook 17.0 million, YouTube 12.1 million, LinkedIn 6.30 million, Snapchat 4.99 million, Instagram 3.95 million, X 2.09 million (DataReportal, 8 Nov 2025) [AFE-46].
  - The Reuters Institute (16 Jun 2026) calls WhatsApp especially influential for political information and rumours. It says TikTok and YouTube are the main entry points for younger audiences [AFE-5].
  - Payments: M-Pesa is the documented channel for paying influencers [AFE-1]. Kenya has 95.31 million mobile-money accounts and 563,605 active agents (August 2026) [AFE-51].
- **Politics & elections 2026–27**
  - The general election is on Tuesday 10 August 2027. Public officers who want to stand must resign by 9 February 2027, and the campaign period runs from 29 May to 7 August 2027 (The Star, 24 Jun 2026) [AFE-40].
  - The KAS study (reported 21 Jun 2026) says influencers and digital operatives are paid to push narratives and attack opponents, at about $10–15 (Sh1,300–1,950) per campaign [AFE-3].
- **Disinformation industry model:** mainly commercial and contracted by political actors, run through WhatsApp marketplaces [AFE-1][AFE-2][AFE-3]. The state is also formalising paid influencer work: a draft National Communication Strategy budgets up to Sh100 million a year (Business Daily, 18 Jan 2026) [AFE-4].
- **Legal risks to victims**
  - **Jailed in Myanmar:** 39 Kenyans were sentenced to one year with labour at Hpa-An Prison under paragraph 13(1) of Myanmar's 1947 Immigration Act, for illegal entry and scam work. They were sentenced on 21 January 2026 (Daily Nation, 22 Mar 2026) [AFE-14].
  - **Detained in Thailand:** 66 Kenyans were in Thailand's Immigration Detention Centre in December 2025 [AFE-12].
  - **Arrested on re-entry:** one man deported earlier re-entered via Malaysia and Thailand and was arrested at Mae Sot for a forged immigration stamp [AFE-13].
  - **Suspected of complicity at home:** Prime CS Mudavadi said in November 2025 that some of those rescued are part of criminal networks, not victims [AFE-13].
  - **Less help home:** families must now buy their own return tickets [AFE-12].
- **Consular reach**
  - Kenya has an embassy in Bangkok, which handles Myanmar cases [AFE-10][AFE-13], and an embassy in Moscow.
  - Its honorary consul in Kyiv has visited one Kenyan prisoner of war [AFE-18].
  - Officials have little access to the 39 prisoners held in remote, militia-controlled Hpa-An [AFE-14].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment

- **Mozilla, "Inside the Shadowy World of Disinformation-for-hire in Kenya" (Madung & Obilo):** found at least 11 campaigns, more than 23,000 tweets and 3,700 accounts (Mozilla Foundation, 2 Sep 2021) [AFE-1].
  - *Recruitment:* anonymous organisers ran WhatsApp groups and sent cash, content and exact posting instructions [AFE-1].
  - *Pay:* one interviewee said influencers earned about US$10–15 for taking part in three campaigns a day, paid directly by M-Pesa [AFE-1].
  - *Account rental:* one influencer said owners of verified "blue tick" accounts rent them out for a cut. The account holders the researchers contacted denied this [AFE-1].
  - *Targets:* the Building Bridges Initiative constitutional process, the Linda Katiba movement, judges and journalists [AFE-1].
  - *Did the workers know?* The report does not say whether participants knew the purpose of each campaign [AFE-1].
  - *Platform response:* Twitter acted against more than 100 Kenyan accounts for platform manipulation and spam [AFE-1].
- **Mozilla follow-up on the Pandora Papers:** more than 10,000 tweets and 2,000 inauthentic accounts pushed #phonyleaks and #offshoreaccountfacts to trend in defence of President Kenyatta (Mozilla Foundation, 3 Nov 2021) [AFE-2].
  - Influencers were recruited through WhatsApp groups that worked as disinformation-for-hire marketplaces. They received regular payments and instructions on what to post and when [AFE-2].
  - *Did the workers know?* The influencers interviewed openly described the work as a business, so they knew they were being paid to push political content. One said he had done it for six years without being caught [AFE-2].
- **Konrad-Adenauer-Stiftung, "The Rise of Fake News Ahead of the 2027 Kenyan Elections":** describes disinformation-for-hire as a commercial industry. Influencers earn about $10 (Sh1,300) to $15 (Sh1,950) per campaign and receive instructions in WhatsApp groups. TikTok, WhatsApp, X, Facebook and YouTube are named as the main channels (The Star, 21 Jun 2026) [AFE-3].
- **State paid-influencer plan:** the draft National Communication Strategy budgets up to Sh100 million a year (Business Daily, 18 Jan 2026) [AFE-4].
  - Initial tiers: 10 "macro" influencers (more than 100,000 followers) at Sh100,000 per quarter, and 20 "micro" influencers (10,000–100,000 followers) at Sh50,000 per quarter [AFE-4].
  - Listed duties include creating hashtags and promoting them [AFE-4].
  - The Reuters Institute's 2026 Kenya page also notes the plan to pay influencers [AFE-5].
- UNVERIFIED (snippet only): Amnesty International reportedly found the government paid bloggers to drown out #RejectFinanceBill protest hashtags in 2024–25 (as summarised by Weetracker). The underlying report was not opened.

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment

**Myanmar scam compounds**
- **February 2025:** 24 Kenyans were among 260 foreigners handed to the Thai army on 12 February. Another 46 were rescued on 19 February, and 4 escaped to Thailand on 8 February (Ministry communiqué, Citizen Digital, 3 Mar 2025) [AFE-10].
- **March 2025:** 64 Kenyans were waiting at the border, part of more than 7,000 foreigners of 30+ nationalities released by the DKBA and BGF armed groups [AFE-10].
- **The lure (survivor account):** customer-service work in Thailand needing good English and typing [AFE-11].
  - The offer came through a friend, and he paid an agent KSh 150,000 [AFE-11].
  - He flew to Bangkok, was driven about 17 hours, and was taken across a river into Myanmar [AFE-11].
  - Inside, he worked on fake-property and crypto "pig-butchering" scams aimed at Americans, with a team target of $10,000 a day and shifts of up to 19 hours, unpaid [AFE-11].
- **22 December 2025:** 119 Kenyans repatriated after Myanmar's raids. 198 were still waiting: 66 in Thai immigration detention, 129 in Myanmar shelters and 3 in a Caritas safe house in Cambodia (Kenyans.co.ke; The EastAfrican) [AFE-12][AFE-13].
  - More than 200 Kenyans had sheltered in military shelters in Myawaddy and Shwe Kokko [AFE-12].
  - 86 Kenyans in the shelters at first refused to cooperate and demanded state-paid tickets [AFE-12].
- **October 2025 to January 2026:** at least 357 Kenyans escaped Myanmar compounds and 253 were repatriated. 39 are jailed in Myanmar (Daily Nation; Mwakilishi, 22 Mar 2026) [AFE-14][AFE-15].
  - Many travelled through Malaysia, Uganda, Tanzania or Ethiopia to avoid checks at Nairobi's JKIA airport [AFE-14].
  - The Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) has arrested several recruiting suspects, and some cases are in court [AFE-14].
  - The Nation page also showed a related teaser about a "digital marketing" job paying Sh100,000 a month [AFE-14].
- UNVERIFIED (snippet only): Mudavadi reportedly told Parliament that more than 751 Kenyans were rescued in Myanmar between 2022 and 2026.

**Russian army**
- **25 September 2025 raid:** the Anti-Terrorism Police Unit rescued more than 20 men (a reporter counted 25) from apartments in Athi River who were bound for Russia. At least two suspects were arrested (The Star, 25 Sep 2025) [AFE-16].
  - One man had been promised Sh250,000 for a "military job" [AFE-16].
  - Another had paid agents Sh53,000 for accommodation and medical checks [AFE-16].
- **February 2026 intelligence report:** a joint report to MPs put the number of Kenyans recruited at more than 1,000, about five times earlier official estimates (Al Jazeera, 16 Mar 2026) [AFE-17].
- **16 March 2026 agreement:** after Mudavadi–Lavrov talks, Russia agreed that Kenyans would no longer be enlisted through its Defence Ministry. Lavrov maintained that the Kenyans signed voluntarily [AFE-17].
- **Mudavadi to the National Assembly (Citizen Digital, 1 Apr 2026)** [AFE-18]:
  - 252 Kenyans recruited, 47 repatriated, 2 held as prisoners of war in Ukraine.
  - Promised pay: KSh 1.5–3 million on joining, plus KSh 50,000–350,000 a month, for "logistics" or combat roles.
  - 600 rogue agencies deregistered.
  - The penalty for illegal recruitment is a KSh 50,000 fine or three months in jail, and is under review.
  - A planned 24-hour call centre will let people verify overseas jobs.
- **Earlier government estimate:** about 200 Kenyans recruited; more than 30 evacuated from Russia in two months; 600 agencies shut. The page header date is 10 Feb 2026 but the URL path reads 2026/08/06, so the date is uncertain (The Moscow Times) [AFE-19].

**Alabuga (drone factory)**
- A Kenyan woman appears in Alabuga Start promotional video (VOA, 18 Dec 2024) [AFE-35].
- No verified Kenya-specific numbers. UNVERIFIED (snippet only): GI-TOC reports the DCI questioned immigration officials about how Alabuga recruits got passports.

### 2.2 Uganda

#### a) Vulnerability factors

- **Jobs & money**
  - Youth unemployment (modelled ILO): 4.2% in 2025. This is misleadingly low because it counts only open unemployment [AFE-43].
  - NEET: 13.8% in 2021 [AFE-44].
  - Vulnerable employment: 74.4% of total employment in 2025, which points to a mostly informal labour market (World Bank WDI) [AFE-45].
  - Remittances: 4.51% of GDP in 2025, about US$2.80 billion, roughly double the 2024 level of US$1.43 billion [AFE-41][AFE-42].
  - The labour minister put the previous year's remittances at about US$1.3 billion (Pulse Uganda, 5 Feb 2026) [AFE-59].
  - Household debt: not researched (gap).
- **Migration & agencies**
  - More than 280,000 jobs have come through labour externalisation since 2016. Most licensed companies place workers in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar and Kuwait (Minister Esther Anyakun, Pulse Uganda, 5 Feb 2026) [AFE-59].
  - In February 2026, 246 companies were licensed and the minister warned that tour and travel firms have no legal right to place workers abroad [AFE-59].
  - 228 companies were listed as licensed on 7 April 2026. 275 licences were revoked on 20 April 2026 for exploitation and fraud (Pulse Uganda, 20 Apr 2026) [AFE-60].
  - The US TIP Report 2025 says agencies may still charge up to UGX 20,000 (about $5) in admin fees plus placement and travel costs. Police closed 16 labour-export companies, and illegal agencies continue to operate [AFE-29].
- **Who is targeted**
  - **Russian army:** men with military or security experience. A recruiter targeted members of the 20,000-strong Special Returnees Association, Ugandans who previously worked as security guards in Iraq and Afghanistan (AFP via Africanews, 13 Feb 2026) [AFE-20].
  - **Alabuga:** women aged 18–22 [AFE-29].
  - **Scam compounds:** both sexes. One 2025 group was 13 men and 13 women (Uganda MoFA, 26 May 2025) [AFE-23].
  - Digital access is low: social-media user identities equal 4.6% of the population and internet users 22.0% (DataReportal, Oct 2025) [AFE-47]. Offers therefore travel through WhatsApp groups, friends and agents more than open platforms.
- **Platforms & payments**
  - Advertising audiences in late 2025: Facebook 2.40 million, LinkedIn 1.80 million, Instagram 883,000, X 789,000 [AFE-47].
  - WhatsApp groups are the main paid-messaging channel. Group admins reportedly charge UGX 100,000 a day to circulate campaign messages (Minister Betty Amongi, reported by Pulse Uganda, 27 Apr 2025) [AFE-8].
  - Mobile money (MTN MoMo, Airtel Money): UNVERIFIED (no source opened).
- **Politics & elections**
  - Museveni won a seventh term on 15 January 2026 with 71.65% (7,946,772 votes). Bobi Wine took 24.72% and rejected the result. Turnout was 52.5% (The EastAfrican, Jan 2026) [AFE-36].
  - The communications regulator (UCC) ordered a nationwide internet shutdown from 13 January 2026, 18:00 local time, to 17 January, lasting 4 days 5 hours. Starlink was switched off on 1 January (Internet Society Pulse) [AFE-37].
  - The next general election is due in 2031. No national vote falls in 2026–27.
- **Disinformation industry model:** state-centred, with a commercial fringe.
  - In 2021 staff at the Government Citizens Interaction Centre (GCIC) were reportedly paid to create fake accounts. One employee said he created more than 40 (ISS, 4 Nov 2026) [AFE-7].
  - In 2026 both the ruling NRM and the opposition NUP used "buzzer" fake accounts, often run by paid operators [AFE-7].
  - ISS also describes a lucrative "influence-for-hire" market [AFE-7].
- **Legal risks to victims**
  - In 2025, rescued Ugandans were held for months in camps run by the DKBA and BGF armed groups while waiting for flights. Activists said Thailand needed Uganda to provide flights first (Daily Monitor, 9 Apr 2025) [AFE-26].
  - Ugandans in the Russian army: State Minister Okello Oryem said the government cannot intervene [AFE-20]. Ambassador Kizige said the men signed contracts with private security companies (Nile Post via AllAfrica, 25 Jul 2026) [AFE-22].
  - Penalties for paid posters: not researched (gap).
- **Consular reach**
  - The High Commission in Kuala Lumpur covers both Thailand and Myanmar [AFE-23][AFE-24].
  - In April 2025 more than 50 Ugandans were stranded and Myanmar had cleared only 16 to leave (MoFA KL, 22 Apr 2025) [AFE-25].
  - Kenya has given consular help to some Ugandans [AFE-13].
  - In July 2026 a Permanent Secretary's letter alleged that embassy staff in Moscow handed citizens seeking help over to Russian authorities [AFE-22].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment

- **2021 Facebook takedown (government-linked):** on 8 January 2021 Facebook removed 32 Pages, 220 accounts, 59 Groups and 139 Instagram profiles for coordinated inauthentic behaviour on behalf of a government entity (DFRLab, 10 Feb 2021) [AFE-6].
  - At least 6 government employees and 2 PR firms were involved: Robusto Communications and White Bear Communications [AFE-6].
  - The government's media centre confirmed the removed accounts belonged to government employees [AFE-6].
  - Twitter removed overlapping accounts three days later [AFE-6].
  - *Did the workers know?* These were staff accounts, so they knew they were posting. ISS later reported that GCIC staff were paid to run fake accounts [AFE-7].
- **2026 election, ISS/Karen Allen International study (ISS, 4 Nov 2026)** [AFE-7]:
  - "The Longest Online Rally": a network of at least nine nodes repackaged material, including deepfakes.
  - "Jazz with Jajja": an event that reportedly filled its audience with paid influencers posing as ordinary citizens.
  - State-aligned influencers took over a popular entertainment YouTube channel to promote #ProtectingTheGains.
  - Both parties used paid fake "buzzer" accounts.
  - *Did the workers know?* The paid influencers knowingly posed as ordinary citizens.
- **WhatsApp admins for hire:** young people build large WhatsApp groups and sell message circulation to candidates, reportedly for UGX 100,000 a day. An admin with several groups could reportedly earn about UGX 1 million a day (Minister Betty Amongi, reported second-hand, Pulse Uganda, 27 Apr 2025) [AFE-8].

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment

**Myanmar scam compounds**
- **Returns:**
  - 23 Ugandans on 23 May 2024 [AFE-26].
  - 6 on 9 March 2025, after 13 months in compounds (Daily Monitor) [AFE-27].
  - 18 handed over at the Mae Sot Friendship Bridge on 28 April 2025 (MoFA KL) [AFE-24].
  - 26 on 19 May 2025 at Myawaddy, the fourth rescued group of 2025, lured by fake job offers in Thailand (MoFA KL) [AFE-23].
- **Stranded:** at least 40 in April 2025 (the ministry listed about 50), held in DKBA/BGF camps after their 18 February rescue [AFE-26]. 25 were still stuck at the border in May 2025 (Daily Monitor, 14 May 2025) [AFE-28].
- **Officials charged:** the US TIP Report 2025 says two Ugandan government officials were charged with fraudulently recruiting Ugandans for "technology jobs" in Thailand. The workers ended up in crypto scams in Burma aimed at Western victims [AFE-29].

**Russian army**
- A Ugandan intelligence source said more than 100 Ugandans with military experience were secretly recruited (AFP via Africanews, 13 Feb 2026) [AFE-20].
  - 9 men were stopped at Entebbe in August 2025 [AFE-20].
  - One recruiter offered veterans jobs in Israel but sent them to Russia [AFE-20].
  - A former cleaner was promised a supermarket or security-guard job, then made to sign an army contract [AFE-20].
- **Embassy letter of 17 October 2026 (Reuters via Daily Sabah, 30 Oct 2026)** [AFE-21]:
  - 27 Ugandans killed or presumed dead: 11 deaths confirmed by Russia, 16 unaccounted for.
  - 5 still under contract, out of about 35 the embassy believed were serving.
  - Lures reported more broadly: well-paid jobs as drivers, security guards, construction workers and cooks.
- **Families' account (Nile Post via AllAfrica, 25 Jul 2026)** [AFE-22]:
  - More than 90 Ugandans are missing.
  - Recruits were promised $6,000 a month for driving or security work.
  - One group went by bus to Juba, then flew to Istanbul and Moscow.
  - They signed contracts in Russian that they could not read.

**Alabuga (drone factory)**
- Russian officials and illicit recruiters recruited Ugandan women aged 18–22 for "vocational training", then placed them at drone-production sites (US TIP Report 2025) [AFE-29].
- AP reported recruiting events in Uganda and attempts to recruit from orphanages. The gender and labour ministry said it raised concerns about the women's ages with its embassy in Moscow (AP investigation of 10 Oct 2024, summarised by The Moscow Times) [AFE-34].
- Uganda's Ministry of Education and Sport shared an Alabuga "scholarship" announcement in 2023 (VOA, 18 Dec 2024) [AFE-35].
- UNVERIFIED (snippet only): Alabuga's Facebook page said 46 Ugandan women were there (AP). A Ugandan MP put the number at about 60 (GI-TOC).

### 2.3 Ethiopia

#### a) Vulnerability factors

- **Jobs & money**
  - Youth unemployment (modelled ILO): 5.4% in 2025. This understates the problem [AFE-43].
  - NEET: 17.5% in 2021 [AFE-44].
  - Vulnerable employment: 84.2% of total employment in 2025, the highest of the three countries [AFE-45].
  - Recorded remittances jumped from 0.40% of GDP (US$0.54 billion) in 2023 to 4.77% (US$7.14 billion) in 2024 (World Bank WDI) [AFE-41][AFE-42]. The jump likely reflects more money moving through formal channels after the 2024 currency reform. That explanation is UNVERIFIED and should not be read as a seven-fold rise in migration.
  - Household debt: not researched (gap).
- **Migration & agencies**
  - Saudi Arabia is the dominant corridor. More than 26,000 nationals were repatriated from Saudi Arabia in five months, alongside 1,300 from Myanmar (Foreign Minister Gedion Timothewos to parliament, The EastAfrican, ~1 Jan 2026) [AFE-33].
  - The new Overseas Employment Proclamation No. 1389/2025 (replacing No. 923/2016) and Directive No. 1126/2026 set up agency licensing tiers, an Overseas Employment Board, recruitment rules aligned with ILO Convention 181, and a central digital system, E-LMIS (ILO, 31 Jul 2026) [AFE-67].
  - Top-tier agencies must have 20 million birr in capital and a US$250,000 deposit, and must report deployments to E-LMIS every quarter (Addis Fortune, 18 Oct 2025) [AFE-68].
  - Officials blame illegal brokers for the Myanmar cases and warn against working in countries with no labour agreement with Ethiopia (Xinhua, 30 May 2025) [AFE-32].
- **Who is targeted**
  - Young job seekers deceived by brokers offering jobs in Southeast Asia [AFE-32][AFE-33].
  - AP reported that Ethiopia's education ministry promoted the Alabuga programme as a way to earn money and learn skills [AFE-34].
  - Only 7.2% of the population had social-media identities in October 2025 (DataReportal) [AFE-48]. Offers therefore spread through closed groups and brokers.
- **Platforms & payments**
  - Advertising audiences in late 2025: Facebook 9.80 million, LinkedIn 1.80 million, Instagram 1.55 million, X 303,000 [AFE-48].
  - Telegram is widely described as Ethiopia's dominant messaging app. Addis Insight (13 Jun 2022) said Telegram is downloaded more than Messenger, WhatsApp or imo in Ethiopia, and cited its low data use and groups of up to 100,000 members [AFE-49]. No recent user survey was verified.
  - Payments: telebirr had 60.6 million users in the 2025/26 fiscal year, with ETB 4.19 trillion across 2.61 billion transactions. Ethio Telecom had 90.12 million customers (TechAfrica News, 23 Jul 2026) [AFE-50].
- **Politics & elections**
  - The 7th general election was held on 1 June 2026, with official results announced on 21 June [AFE-38][AFE-39].
  - The Prosperity Party won 438 of 486 seats by AFP's count. Voting took place in 501 of 547 constituencies [AFE-38].
  - Tigray did not vote. Polls did not open in eight Amhara constituencies, and the conflict monitor ACLED recorded at least 90 clashes on polling day (AFP via Arab News) [AFE-38].
  - Before the vote, INSA warned of WhatsApp hijacking scams that used fake party links (AllAfrica, 27 Feb 2026) [AFE-69].
  - The next national election is due in 2031.
- **Disinformation industry model:** mostly state-run. The one platform-attributed network was linked to INSA [AFE-9]. Commercial disinformation-for-hire was not documented in sources opened (gap).
- **Legal risks to victims**
  - Myanmar framed 90 Ethiopians it deported on 14 May 2025 as "linked to online fraud". The same report says 11 other foreigners were detained for illegal entry (Eleven Myanmar, 15 May 2025) [AFE-31].
  - At home, officials describe the victims as people who chose illegal overseas work [AFE-32][AFE-33].
  - Penalties for online speech (for example the 2020 hate-speech and disinformation law): UNVERIFIED, no source opened.
- **Consular reach:** the government relied on its embassy in India to move 133 nationals into Thailand, and on its embassy in Japan (ENA, 10 Mar 2025) [AFE-30]. The New Delhi mission was tasked with ongoing repatriation (Xinhua, 30 May 2025) [AFE-32].

#### b) Documented disinformation-for-hire / troll recruitment

- **2021 Meta takedown (security-agency-linked):** Meta removed 62 Facebook accounts, 49 Pages, 26 Groups and 32 Instagram accounts, citing links to individuals associated with INSA (Meta, 16 Jun 2021, updated 7 Jul 2021) [AFE-9].
  - About 1.1 million accounts followed the Pages and about 766,000 joined the Groups. Ad spend was about $7,200 [AFE-9].
  - Content was mostly in Amharic: praise for the Prosperity Party and Abiy Ahmed, attacks on opposition groups (Oromo Liberation Front, Ethiopian Democratic Party, TPLF), and criticism of Egypt and Sudan over the Nile dam [AFE-9].
  - Tactics included fake and duplicate accounts and Pages posing as media outlets [AFE-9].
  - *Recruitment, pay and awareness:* not disclosed [AFE-9].
- No verified commercial "per post" market was found for Ethiopia (gap). The INSA warning shows party branding being used as bait for account takeover, a nearby risk for young people paid to "support" parties online [AFE-69].

#### c) Scam-compound trafficking & other deceptive recruitment

**Myanmar scam compounds**
- **10 March 2025:** 32 Ethiopians repatriated, with 43 more due that night, out of 133 moved to Thailand through the embassy in India. The foreign ministry warned against traffickers' fake job promises (ENA via AllAfrica) [AFE-30].

**Russian army and Alabuga**
- AP says the education ministry promoted Alabuga (via The Moscow Times) [AFE-34]. No numbers.

## 3. Synthesis for job seekers in East Africa & the Horn

### 3.1 What to know

1. **A real overseas job needs two checks: the agency and the specific job.**
   - Kenya: check both on the NEA's NEAIMS portal [AFE-54].
   - Uganda: check both on EEMIS (eemis.mglsd.go.ug) [AFE-57][AFE-59].
   - Ethiopia: only licensed agencies can register you on E-LMIS [AFE-67][AFE-68].
   - In Uganda, tour and travel companies and individual "agents" may not place you abroad at all [AFE-59].
2. **"Customer care / typing / digital marketing job in Thailand, ticket paid" is the classic scam-compound lure** [AFE-11][AFE-14][AFE-23]. Warning signs:
   - you are told to travel on a tourist visa;
   - you are told to fly out via Uganda, Tanzania, Ethiopia or Malaysia to avoid airport checks [AFE-12][AFE-14];
   - you are driven for hours from Bangkok toward the border.

   This is how people end up in Myanmar compounds, working 16–19 hour shifts unpaid [AFE-11][AFE-26].
3. **A security-guard, driver, cook, supermarket or "military logistics" job in Russia can mean the front line.** The same goes for a job in "Israel" or "Europe" that routes through Moscow.
   - Recruits were promised KSh 1.5–3 million on signing or $6,000 a month [AFE-18][AFE-22].
   - Contracts were in Russian [AFE-22], and 27 Ugandans are dead or presumed dead [AFE-21].
   - Your government may say it cannot help because you "signed a contract" [AFE-20][AFE-22].
4. **Young women: a Russian "work and study" or "scholarship" offer can lead to drone-factory work** (Alabuga Start) [AFE-29][AFE-35]. This is true even when a ministry shares the offer [AFE-34][AFE-35].
5. **Large agent fees are a red flag, not proof the job is real.** One victim paid KSh 150,000 [AFE-11]. Another paid Sh53,000 for "medicals and accommodation" before a planned trip to Russia [AFE-16]. In Uganda the legal admin fee is capped at UGX 20,000 [AFE-29].
6. **"Online jobs" paying per post, per hashtag or per day in a WhatsApp group are often paid political propaganda.**
   - Pay is around US$10–15 per campaign, sent by M-Pesa [AFE-1][AFE-3].
   - In Uganda, WhatsApp group admins reportedly charge UGX 100,000 a day [AFE-8].
   - Demand will peak before Kenya's 10 August 2027 vote [AFE-40].
   - If the client is anonymous and you only get a hashtag and a script, you are part of a campaign.
7. **If you are trapped, you may be treated as a suspect.**
   - 39 Kenyans are in a Myanmar prison [AFE-14] and dozens have been held in Thai immigration detention [AFE-12].
   - Kenya now asks families to pay for return tickets [AFE-12].
   - Keep copies of your offer letter, chats and payment receipts. They help show you were deceived.
8. **Save these numbers before you travel:**
   - Uganda trafficking: 0800-199-003 / 0800-199-004 (toll-free) [AFE-61]
   - Uganda High Commission in Kuala Lumpur (covers Thailand and Myanmar): +603-2078-2420 [AFE-23]
   - Ethiopia trafficking: 8797 (toll-free) [AFE-65]
   - Ethiopia police: 991 [AFE-66]
   - Kenyan numbers: see the other researcher's hotline list.
