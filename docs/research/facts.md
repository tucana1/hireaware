# HireAware fact sheet: disinformation-for-hire and scam-compound recruitment

Main focus: the Philippines. Secondary: Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, India, Indonesia, Sri Lanka, Malaysia, Thailand, plus Russia's Internet Research Agency and Israel-based "Team Jorge".

Compiled 7 November 2026. Every URL below was opened on 2026-11-07 unless it is marked otherwise.

## How to read this sheet

- **Format.** Each fact gives the claim in our own words, the exact figure, the publisher, the publication date and the URL that was opened. Short quotes are kept under 12 words.
- **UNVERIFIED** means one of three things. The figure or contact could not be confirmed on a page we opened; it appeared only in search-result snippets; or the official site blocked automated access.
- **Blocked or unreachable official sites.** Many Philippine government sites showed a bot-check page or refused the connection when we tried to open them:
  - iacat.gov.ph
  - cicc.gov.ph
  - pia.gov.ph
  - acg.pnp.gov.ph
  - sec.gov.ph
  - 1343actionline.ph
  - the DFA embassy sites (yangonpe.dfa.gov.ph, bangkokpe.dfa.gov.ph)
  - immigration.gov.ph (unreachable)

  We did not try to get past these checks. **A person should confirm every UNVERIFIED contact by phone or in a normal browser before publishing.**
- **Repatriation counts are not additive.** Agencies count them differently: DFA, DMW, BI, the NAIA airport task force and embassy police attachés each keep their own tallies, by date of arrival, request or rescue. Do not add them together.
- **Search budget.** The web-search budget ran out near the end of the research. The gaps this left are listed in section 8.

## 1. Key statistics

### 1.1 Scam compounds: global and regional scale

- **OHCHR, 2023 baseline.** The UN Human Rights Office estimated that at least 120,000 people in Myanmar and about 100,000 in Cambodia may be held and forced to run online scams. It named Lao PDR, the Philippines and Thailand as other main destination or transit countries.
  - **Figure:** 120,000 (Myanmar); 100,000 (Cambodia)
  - **Publisher:** UN News, reporting the OHCHR report
  - **Date:** 29 Aug 2023
  - **URL:** https://news.un.org/en/story/2023/08/1140187
  - The OHCHR press release itself (ohchr.org) blocked automated access, so the UN News version was used.

- **OHCHR, 2026 update ("A wicked problem").** At least 300,000 people work in scam operations across Southeast Asia. Victims come from 66 countries (count as of March 2025). About 74% of compounds are in the Mekong region.
  - **Figure:** 300,000+ people; 66 origin countries; 74% in the Mekong
  - **Revenue estimates:** about US$64 billion a year worldwide, and more than US$43.8 billion a year in the Mekong alone
  - **Publisher:** UN News
  - **Date:** 20 Feb 2026
  - **URL:** https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/02/1167012

- **OHCHR press release on the same report.**
  - Survivors interviewed had been trafficked into Cambodia, Lao PDR, Myanmar, **the Philippines** and the UAE between 2021 and 2025.
  - Some compounds cover more than 500 acres.
  - Every interviewee had wages cut through deductions, and none received the full promised salary.
  - **Publisher:** OHCHR
  - **Date:** 20 Feb 2026
  - **URL:** https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2026/02/un-report-details-grave-abuses-against-those-trafficked-scam-centres

The networks are spreading to Africa, Latin America, South Asia, the Middle East and Pacific islands. The Philippines is named alongside Cambodia, Laos and Myanmar as a place where compounds multiplied.
  - **Figure:** just under US$40 billion a year
  - **Publisher:** Associated Press via The Star (Malaysia)
  - **Date:** 22 Apr 2025 (the report was released 21 Apr 2025)
  - **URL:** https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2025/04/22/un-researchers-warn-that-asian-scam-operations-are-spreading-across-the-rest-of-the-world

  - **Publisher:** UNODC
  - **Date:** 21 Jul 2026

### 1.2 Disinformation-for-hire: global scale

- **Oxford Internet Institute, 2020 global inventory** ("Industrialized Disinformation", Bradshaw, Bailey & Howard).
  - Organised social-media manipulation was found in 81 countries, up from 70.
  - State actors worked with private firms in 48 countries.
  - Almost US$60 million has been spent on such firms since 2009.
  - More than 65 firms have offered computational propaganda as a service since 2018.
  - Facebook and Twitter removed more than 317,000 accounts and pages.
  - **Publisher:** Oxford Internet Institute (press release) and OII DemTech (report page)
  - **Date:** 13 Jan 2021
  - **URLs:**
    - https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/social-media-manipulation-by-political-actors-now-an-industrial-scale-problem-prevalent-in-over-80-countries-annual-oxford-report/
    - https://demtech.oii.ox.ac.uk/research/posts/industrialized-disinformation/
  - **Discrepancies:** the press release dates the account removals January 2019 to **November** 2020, while the DemTech page says to **December** 2020. The 48-countries figure comes from the press release; the DemTech page says "48 instances".

### 1.5 Philippines: the POGO ban, step by step

- **22 Jul 2024, State of the Nation Address.** President Marcos declared: "Effective today, all POGOs are banned." He told the gaming regulator PAGCOR to wind down POGO operations by the end of 2024. The PTV report ties POGOs to scamming, money laundering, trafficking, kidnapping, torture and murder.
  - **Publisher:** PTV News
  - **Date:** 22 Jul 2024
  - **URL:** https://ptvnews.ph/pbbm-orders-ban-of-pogos-in-ph/

- **5 Nov 2024, Executive Order No. 74.**
  - The order immediately banned POGOs, internet gaming licensees (IGLs) and other offshore gaming licensees.
  - It took effect on publication.
  - It created a working group against illegal offshore gaming, chaired by the Presidential Anti-Organized Crime Commission (PAOCC), and a second group on re-employing displaced workers.
  - **Publisher:** PTV News
  - **Date:** 8 Nov 2024
  - **URL:** https://ptvnews.ph/pbbm-orders-immediate-ban-of-pogos-igls-other-offshore-gaming-ops/
  - **UNVERIFIED:** the article we opened gives no 31 Dec 2024 cessation deadline. That date appears only in secondary coverage.

- **23 Oct 2025, Republic Act 12312 (Anti-POGO Act of 2025).**
  - It bans offshore gaming outright and repeals RA 11590.
  - **First offence:** 6–8 years in prison and a fine of at least ₱15 million. Probation is not available.
  - **Section 9:** recruiting people for offshore gaming under the pretext of a job, training or apprenticeship is treated as trafficking under RA 10364.
  - **Section 6:** all POGO-related work permits and visas are revoked.
  - It takes effect 15 days after publication.
  - **Publisher:** LawPhil (statute text)
  - **Date:** approved 23 Oct 2025
  - **URL:** https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2025/ra_12312_2025.html
  - **Also:** PTV News, 29 Oct 2025, https://ptvnews.ph/?p=246912
  - **Discrepancy:** PTV gives the first-offence fine as ₱300,000–₱15 million. Use the statute text.

## 2. How recruitment works

### 2A. Disinformation and troll recruitment

**Who recruits, and who gets recruited (Philippines)**

- **A professional hierarchy.**
  - **Top:** advertising and PR strategists, the "chief architects", set campaign goals for political clients.
  - **Middle:** anonymous digital influencers who run pages with 50,000 to 2,000,000 followers.
  - **Bottom:** community-level fake-account operators who meet daily quotas of posts and comments.
  - **Why people join:** money worries and career ambitions.
  - **Day jobs:** many keep them, as ad or PR executives, programmers or politicians' administrative staff.
  - **Research base:** the study interviewed 20 disinformation architects between December 2016 and December 2017.
  - **Publisher:** Ong & Cabañes, *Architects of Networked Disinformation*, via University of Leeds and Philstar
  - **Date:** 12 Feb 2018
  - **URLs:**
    - https://www.leeds.ac.uk/news/article/4184/fake_news_production_and_social_media_trolls
    - https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2018/02/12/1787103/ad-pr-execs-are-chief-architects-disinformation-philippines-study
    - Repository record: https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/127312/

- **Four work models.** The authors' follow-up report sets out four models:
  - in-house staff
  - advertising and PR firms
  - clickbait producers
  - state-sponsored campaigns (described from secondary sources)
  - **Publisher:** Ong & Cabañes, NATO StratCom Centre of Excellence, via Goldsmiths Research Online
  - **Date:** 2019
  - **URL:** https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/37415

- **Who troll farms hire.** They draw on the same young, educated, English-speaking workforce that staffs call centres and content moderation.
  - Workers do eight-hour shifts.
  - New accounts are "cultivated" for about 15 days before they post.
  - Some firms said they had foreign clients, including from Britain.
  - **Publisher:** The Washington Post, via the Information Professionals Association
  - **Date:** 26 Jul 2019
  - **URL:** https://information-professionals.org/why-crafty-internet-trolls-in-the-philippines-may-be-coming-to-a-website-near-you/

- **Daily work.** Trolls ran up to 200 personas each, across three shifts. A PR head and content editor set the narratives. The leader said they mostly "boost" and "defend" candidates.
  - **Publisher:** GMA News
  - **Date:** 27 Feb 2019
  - **URL:** https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/686376/ph-online-troll-earns-between-p30-000-p70-000-a-month-leader-bares/story/

- **2025: a marketing firm as covert vendor ("High Five").** OpenAI banned ChatGPT accounts linked to Comm&Sense Inc., a Philippine marketing company.
  - The accounts mass-produced short English and Taglish comments praising President Marcos and attacking Vice-President Sara Duterte.
  - The comments were posted on TikTok and on Facebook replies to mainstream news posts.
  - Five TikTok channels started in mid-February 2025.
  - The commenting accounts had 0–10 followers.
  - The firm also produced sales pitches for this work.
  - **Publisher:** OpenAI, *Disrupting malicious uses of AI: June 2025*
  - **Date:** June 2025
  - **URLs:**
    - Page: https://openai.com/global-affairs/disrupting-malicious-uses-of-ai-june-2025/
    - PDF: https://cdn.openai.com/threat-intelligence-reports/5f73af09-a3a3-4a55-992e-069237681620/disrupting-malicious-uses-of-ai-june-2025.pdf

- **Hidden clients.** In the 2025 Senate allegation (section 1.3), the alleged paying client was a foreign embassy acting through a local PR firm. Workers' fake profiles posed as teachers and OFWs.
  - **Publisher:** PTV News
  - **Date:** 25 Apr 2025
  - **URL:** https://ptvnews.ph/chinese-funded-trolls-other-espionage-threats-bared-in-senate/

**Elsewhere: job titles, channels, and what workers were told versus what they did**

- **Russia, Internet Research Agency job ads (2014–2015).**
  - **Where posted:** Russian job sites hh.ru and SuperJob, later also work-at.me under a new company name.
  - **Job titles:** "social media specialist", "content manager", "web programmer"; in 2015 also copywriters and content creators.
  - **Pay:** 40,000 roubles a month (about US$700) for specialists and content managers; 60,000 roubles for programmers.
  - **What the ads said:** vague duties such as preparing "thematic posts" and "monitoring".
  - **Publisher:** BuzzFeed News
  - **Date:** 22 Feb 2018
  - **URL:** https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/janelytvynenko/job-ads-for-russian-troll-factory

- **Russia, what the Internet Research Agency work actually was.** Yle (Finnish public broadcaster) found:
  - **Job titles advertised:** "Internet operators", content managers and copywriters, on day and night shifts.
  - **Pay:** about €400 a month for regular staff and about €660 for managers.
  - **The real work:** posting propaganda on blogs, forums, VKontakte, Facebook and Twitter, and in media comment sections, often from several profiles at once.
  - **Output:** one troll can produce 50–100 messages a day.
  - **Publisher:** Yle Kioski
  - **Date:** **UNVERIFIED** (the page shows no date; the reporting appears to be from about 2015)

**Common job framings (our synthesis from the sources above)**

- "Social media specialist", "content manager" or "copywriter".
- "Internet operator".
- "Page admin" or "digital influencer".
- Work-from-home "account handling" paid by daily quota.

The real work is running fake or multiple accounts, posting supplied talking points, and boosting or attacking named people. The true client (a politician, party, government or foreign state) is often hidden behind a PR firm, an NGO or a subcontractor.

**Travel routes and visa type**

- **Air route via Mae Sot.**
  - Recruits fly to Thailand, where a Chinese agent meets them.
  - They are driven to Mae Sot and cross the Moei River into Myawaddy, Myanmar.
  - There are no Myanmar entry stamps.
  - **Publisher:** Inquirer via ANN
  - **Date:** 12 Feb 2025
  - **URL:** https://asianews.network/?p=188579
- **Southern "backdoor" route.**

- **Visa type.**

- **Who exits how.**
  - The US TIP report says recruiters often hire Filipinos for supposed jobs in Thailand, then move them by force to scam centres in neighbouring countries.
  - It notes social media, tourist visas and "backdoor" exits as common recruitment and exit methods.
  - **Publisher:** US State Department TIP 2025 (copy on ecoi.net)
  - **Date:** 29 Sep 2025
  - **URL:** https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2130638.html
- **Exit points.** Some victims left through NAIA (Manila's international airport) and others through "backdoor" routes (DZRH, 5 Feb 2026).
- **Who is targeted.** Syndicates increasingly go after middle-class professionals and graduates, often people already employed at home.
  - **Publisher:** Arab News (DOJ spokesperson)
  - **Date:** 5 Apr 2025
  - **URL:** https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2595984/world

**What recruits were told versus what they actually did**

- **Told:** customer service, administrative, IT or marketing jobs, with good pay, free flights and housing.
- **Actually did:**
  - **Romance-investment ("pig-butchering") and crypto scams.**
    - Workers used fake personas built with hired models' photos and scripted chats on dating apps, then steered targets to fake crypto platforms (IJM, 18 May 2026).
    - Workers had set roles: "finders" picked targets on social media and "chatters" built relationships.
    - They posed as European or Asian women and used AI deepfakes on video calls. Payments ran in USDT through a forex platform (ANN, 12 Feb 2025).
  - **Long shifts:**
    - about 15-hour shifts (GMA, 15 Jul 2025)
    - 18-hour days (ANN, 19 May 2025)
    - up to 19-hour shifts (UN News, 20 Feb 2026)
    - up to 20 hours a day, six days a week (IJM forced-scamming page)
  - **Control and abuse:**
    - passports and phones confiscated
    - beatings for missed quotas
    - workers "sold" to other compounds
    - ransoms: ₱270,000 in one case and US$1,500 each in another (ANN, 12 Feb 2025)
  - **Sources:** IJM, https://www.ijm.org/our-work/trafficking-slavery/forced-scamming (undated page); ANN 12 Feb 2025
- **Wages.** Every OHCHR interviewee had pay cut by deductions. One Thai victim faced a target of about US$9,500 a day (OHCHR, 20 Feb 2026).

## 3. Case studies

1. **Architects of Networked Disinformation (Ong & Cabañes, 2018).**
   - A year of interviews and observation showed that Philippine political disinformation was run by advertising and PR strategists, often alongside their regular jobs.
   - The strategists hired middle-tier influencers and low-paid fake-account operators to post to quota.
   - Many workers were drawn in by financial insecurity. Many kept ordinary day jobs.
   - **Sources:** Leeds, 12 Feb 2018 (https://www.leeds.ac.uk/news/article/4184/fake_news_production_and_social_media_trolls); Philstar, 12 Feb 2018 (link in 2A)

2. **Meta takedown of the Nic Gabunada network (March 2019).**
   - Meta removed 67 Pages, 68 Facebook accounts, 40 Groups and 25 Instagram accounts. It linked them to a network organised by Nic Gabunada, Duterte's 2016 social-media strategist.
   - **Reach:** about 3.6 million accounts followed at least one Page, and about 1.8 million joined at least one Group.
   - **Ad spend:** about US$59,000 between January 2014 and March 2019.
   - Meta acted on deceptive behaviour, such as fake accounts and hidden identities, not on what was posted.
   - **Publisher:** Meta
   - **Date:** 28 Mar 2019
   - **URL:** https://about.fb.com/news/2019/03/cib-from-the-philippines/

3. **Meta takedown linked to the Philippine military and police (September 2020).**
   - Meta removed 64 Facebook accounts, 32 Pages and 33 Instagram accounts. It found links to the Philippine military and police.
   - **Reach:** about 209,000 accounts followed the Pages. Ad spend was about US$1,300.
   - **Content:** posts about the pending anti-terrorism bill and military anti-terror operations; criticism of communism and the CPP-NPA-NDF (the communist party, its armed wing and its political front); and posts about youth activists and the opposition.
   - **A second network, from Fujian, China:** 155 accounts, 11 Pages, 9 Groups and 6 Instagram accounts. It praised Duterte, backed a possible 2022 run by Sara Duterte, criticised the news site Rappler and promoted Beijing's South China Sea interests.
   - **Publisher:** Meta
   - **Date:** 22 Sep 2020
   - **URL:** https://about.fb.com/news/2020/09/removing-coordinated-inauthentic-behavior-china-philippines/
   - Some news reports gave lower counts (57/31/20). Meta's own figures are used here.

4. **Later Philippine case: Operation "High Five" (OpenAI, June 2025).** This is a commercial marketing firm doing covert political work during the 2025 midterm season.
   - Comm&Sense Inc. used ChatGPT to analyse posts, mass-produce partisan comments and write sales pitches.
   - The comments ran in the thousands. Almost none drew real engagement.
   - On one ABS-CBN post the firm placed several hundred comments, against about 23,000 in total.
   - OpenAI rated it Category 2 of 6 on its impact scale.
   - **Publisher:** OpenAI
   - **URLs:** see 2A
   - **No later Philippine-specific Meta takedown was found.** The 2022 Meta report covers a Philippine hacktivist network, which is not CIB: https://about.fb.com/news/2022/04/metas-adversarial-threat-report-q1-2022/ (7 Apr 2022).

5. **POGO raids: Clark (2023), Bamban (2024) and Porac (2024).**
   - **Clark Sun Valley Hub, 4 May 2023:** more than 1,000 potential victims freed. In May 2026 three foreign bosses got life sentences (IJM, 18 May 2026).
   - **Bamban, Tarlac, 13 Mar 2024:** 875 people held, 504 of them foreign. Love-scam scripts were seized. The case led to the November 2025 life sentence of former mayor Alice Guo and the forfeiture of the ₱6-billion compound (Philstar, 14 Mar 2024; GMA, 20 Nov 2025).
   - **Porac, Pampanga, June 2024:** 187 rescued, 158 foreign and 29 Filipino (SunStar, 6 Jun 2024).
   - These cases fed the 2024 ban and the 2025 Anti-POGO Act (section 1.5).

6. **Filipinos in Myawaddy compounds, and the 2025 crackdown.**
   - **Early 2025:** after Thailand cut power, fuel and internet to border scam towns, armed groups released thousands of foreigners. The UN puts it at about 7,000.
   - **Late March 2025:** 206 Filipinos were repatriated from Myawaddy (Arab News). The crossing via Mae Sot and the exact batch dates are **UNVERIFIED** (search snippets only).
   - **After the 20 October 2025 raids on KK Park:** 222 Filipinos sought repatriation (Yangon embassy).
   - **12 November 2025:** 346 Filipinos flew home, the largest single batch from Myanmar.
   - **The junta's claims:** 2,198 arrests at KK Park and demolitions at KK Park and Shwe Kokko.
   - **Independent analysts question how complete this was.** Satellite analysis cited by The Straits Times found only 13% of KK Park demolished. **UNVERIFIED:** search snippet only.
   - **Sources:** UN News, 20 Feb 2026; Arab News, 5 Apr 2025; The Star, 26 Oct and 13 Nov 2025; OCCRP, 15 Dec 2025 (URLs in section 1)

7. **Kenya's paid Twitter influencers (Mozilla Foundation, 2021).**
   - Influencers were paid about US$10–15 a day via M-Pesa and coordinated in WhatsApp groups.
   - The campaigns pushed hashtags on the BBI constitutional-change push and targeted judges, journalists, activists and members of the Linda Katiba movement. Some activists reported self-censoring.
   - The researchers analysed 23,000+ tweets from 3,700 accounts. Twitter later removed about 100 accounts (search snippet; **UNVERIFIED**).
   - **Publisher:** Mozilla Foundation
   - **Date:** 2 Sep 2021
   - **URL:** https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/blog/fellow-research-inside-the-shadowy-world-of-disinformation-for-hire-in-kenya/

8. **Russia-linked troll operation in Ghana and Nigeria (March 2020).**
   - Meta removed 49 Facebook accounts, 69 Pages and 85 Instagram accounts run by locals in Ghana and Nigeria on behalf of people in Russia. Meta said some took part knowingly and some did not.
   - It linked the network to the Ghanaian NGO EBLA and to people tied to the Internet Research Agency.
   - **Content:** US race and social issues: Black history, police brutality and LGBTQ topics.
   - **Reach:** about 265,000 people followed the Instagram accounts, about 65% of them in the US.
   - CNN, as summarised by CyberScoop, found young Ghanaian workers coordinating on Telegram from phones they had been given.
   - **Publishers:** Meta, 12 Mar 2020; CyberScoop, 12 Mar 2020 (URLs in 2A)

9. **The Internet Research Agency's ordinary job ads (2014–2015).**
   - The Internet Research Agency advertised "social media specialist" and "content manager" jobs on mainstream Russian job sites. The ads offered about 40,000 roubles a month and described the work in bland terms such as "thematic posts" and "monitoring".
   - In practice, staff posted pro-Kremlin comments and propaganda in shifts, 50–100 messages a day.
   - **Sources:** BuzzFeed News, 22 Feb 2018; Yle Kioski (undated); URLs in 2A

10. **Team Jorge (February 2023).** An Israel-based unit led by Tal Hanan, who used the alias "Jorge", pitched hacking and disinformation services to reporters posing as clients.
    - **How it was exposed:** the reporters met the team between July and December 2022.
    - **Claim:** Hanan said the team had worked on 33 presidential campaigns, 27 of them successful. This is unverified.
    - **Tools:** its "AIMS" platform ran more than 30,000 fake profiles, and one operator could run about 300.
    - **Price:** a campaign was quoted at about €6 million.
    - **Countries named in the reporting:** Kenya, Nigeria and the Philippines, among others.
    - **Response:** Hanan denied wrongdoing.
    - **Publisher:** Forbidden Stories (*Story Killers*)
    - **Date:** 15 Feb 2023
    - **URL:** https://forbiddenstories.org/story-killers/team-jorge-disinformation/

## 4. Official warning signs

### Philippines: Department of Migrant Workers (DMW)

- **2022 advisory** (GMA, 3 Dec 2022, URL in 2B). Warning signs:
  - Social-media ads for customer service, tech support, data-encoder or call-centre jobs in Cambodia, Laos or Myanmar.
  - Free plane tickets.
  - Claims that no visa is needed because ASEAN tourists can enter visa-free.
  - Pressure to leave without DMW processing.
  - **What a legitimate process looks like:** a job offer naming a real company, an interview, a contract, a work visa, and DMW processing.

- **January 2025 alert** (Gulf News, 22 Jan 2025). Warning signs:
  - Copycat pages that clone licensed agencies' official pages.
  - Large fees for jobs that do not exist.
  - Call-centre jobs in Thailand, Singapore or Vietnam that turn out to be crypto-scam work in Cambodia, Myanmar or Laos.
  - Being told to travel as a tourist.

- **May 2026 guidance** (Gulf News, 30 May 2026). Warning signs:
  - High pay, fast processing or immediate deployment in exchange for big fees.
  - Pressure to pay at once.
  - The DMW also cautions that a recruiter looking legitimate does not make the operation legal.

- **May 2026 warning on Cambodia and Thailand scam hubs** (DZRH, 8 May 2026). Warning signs:
  - Recruitment through apps.
  - Unusually high salary offers.
  - "IT" or "customer service" job titles.

### Philippines: Bureau of Immigration (BI)

- **February 2025** (GMA, 21 Feb 2025). Overseas scam hubs work like illegal POGOs. They lure jobseekers on social media with high-paying customer-service jobs abroad.

- **July 2025** (GMA, 15 Jul 2025). The BI warned about "love scam" job offers. In the case it described:
  - the recruit came through a former colleague

### US State Department, Trafficking in Persons Report 2025 (Philippines)

- **Pattern:** recruiters offer jobs in Thailand, then move people to scam centres in Burma, Cambodia and Laos.
- **Methods noted:** social media and digital platforms, tourist visas and "backdoor" exits.
- **The report asks the government to:**
  - proactively identify scam-operation victims

  - build a central database of illegal recruiters

### Platforms (Meta and WhatsApp)

  - Be wary of unrealistic pay and pressure to act fast.
  - Be wary of any "opportunity" that needs money upfront.
  - **URL:** https://about.fb.com/news/2025/08/new-whatsapp-tools-tips-beat-messaging-scams/

### Combined red-flag checklist (our synthesis of the sources above)

**For overseas jobs:**
1. The job is in, or routed through, Thailand, Cambodia, Myanmar or Laos. It is described as customer service, chat support, data encoding, IT or marketing.
2. You are told to travel on a tourist visa, or told you need no visa.
3. You are offered free flights and housing, or high pay for an entry-level job (about US$1,000–2,500 a month or more).
4. The recruiter contacts you through Telegram, Facebook, TikTok or WhatsApp, or through a friend. The agency is not on the DMW list.
5. There is no contract before you leave, or you are rushed to depart.
6. You are told to avoid DMW or airport processing, or to use a "backdoor" route.

**For "social media" jobs:**
7. You are asked to run several accounts or personas, or post scripted comments to a quota.
8. Nobody will tell you who the end client is.

**How to check a recruiter in the Philippines** (DMW guidance via Gulf News, 30 May 2026, plus the official pages above):

1. Confirm the agency is listed in the DMW licensed-agency directory.
2. Confirm the specific job is an approved job order.
3. Check that the business name is registered with DTI (sole proprietorships) or SEC (corporations; tool UNVERIFIED).

## 6. Advice from credible sources

### Questions to ask before accepting a job

- **"Is your agency licensed by the DMW, and is this an approved job order? What is your licence number?"** Check both on dmw.gov.ph (DMW via Gulf News, 30 May 2026; GMA, 3 Dec 2022).
- **"What visa will I travel on?"** A tourist visa, or "no visa needed", is a stop sign (DMW via GMA, 2022, and via Gulf News, 2025 and 2026).
- **"Which country, city and company will I actually work in? Can I see the employer's registration and the full contract before I fly?"** OHCHR found contracts were often shown only after arrival. The DMW says to check the contract matches the advertised terms.
- **"Why is the pay so high for this role? What fees do I pay, and will I get official receipts?"** See IOM (US$1,000–2,500 lure range) and DMW (big fees, pressure to pay, no receipts).
- **For social-media jobs: "Who is the end client? Will I post under my own name, or run other accounts or personas? Will I post supplied scripts to a quota?"** This is our synthesis. Meta's rules prohibit fake or multiple accounts, misrepresenting identity or purpose, hiding who controls a Page, and faking engagement. Ong & Cabañes found that political clients are usually hidden behind PR firms.
  - **Meta Inauthentic Behavior policy:** https://transparency.meta.com/policies/community-standards/inauthentic-behavior/ (accessed 2026-11-07)

### How to check an offer

1. Look up the agency and the job order on DMW (links in section 5), or call 1348.
2. Look up the company on DTI BNRS, or on SEC (tool UNVERIFIED).
3. Outside the Philippines, use the national labour agency:
   - Kenya: National Employment Authority portal www.neamis.go.ke
   - Sri Lanka: SLBFE, 1989
4. Kenyan officials also advise getting offers for Thailand authenticated by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs or the Kenyan embassy in Bangkok (Ministry of Foreign and Diaspora Affairs, 12 Nov 2025; diaspora.go.ke, 6 Apr 2025).
5. Be wary of any "task" job that pays for likes or reviews, moves you to Telegram, then asks for a deposit (Meta/WhatsApp, 5 Aug 2025).

### What evidence to keep

- Copies of all recruitment documents and official receipts for every payment (DMW via Gulf News, 30 May 2026).
- **Our synthesis, consistent with what GASO says it analyses** (survivor accounts, recruitment scripts and wallet data):
  - screenshots of the job ad, with its URL and the date
  - the recruiter's profile links and phone numbers
  - full chat exports
  - the contract
  - tickets and itinerary
  - payment and crypto-wallet records

### If you are already inside a compound (safety first)

- **Contact your embassy or the DMW quietly, if you can.**
  - From abroad: DMW 1348, or the Philippine embassy (numbers UNVERIFIED, see section 5).
  - A Philippine embassy police attaché guided at least one escape from Myawaddy (Inquirer via ANN, 12 Feb 2025).
- **Beware of fake rescuers.** Some Filipinos were cheated by people posing as NGOs who demanded money for "rescue". Some victims paid up to US$3,000 to "escape" (Inquirer via ANN, 19 May 2025).
- **You are a victim, not a criminal.** The UN says forced-scam victims are entitled to protection under the "non-punishment principle" (OHCHR, 20 Feb 2026; IOM explainer).
  - **Caveat:** in practice, enforcement varies. The US TIP report found the Philippines deported at least 3,000 foreigners without identifying them as trafficking victims.
  - **Caveat:** Indonesia's embassy warns that people who keep scamming in Cambodia face tougher prosecution (Kemlu, 1 Jun 2026).
- **Specialist help:** IJM and GASO take referrals (section 5).

### If you suspect your job is propaganda or a troll operation (our synthesis)

- **Do not confront your employer or go public while you are still exposed.** Quietly keep records: briefs, scripts, account lists, payment slips, and the client's name if it is revealed.
- **Check the work against Meta's Inauthentic Behavior rules.** Fake personas, multiple accounts, coordinated posting with hidden sponsorship, and engagement farming are all violations. Platforms have removed whole networks for this, from the Philippines in 2019 and 2020, and Ghana and Nigeria in 2020.
- **Know that the people doing the work are sometimes unaware.** Meta says some Ghanaian and Nigerian operators did not know they were working for Russia-linked actors.
- **Report.** You can report to the platform. In elections, the Philippine election commission (COMELEC) requires candidates to register official social-media accounts (Resolution 11064, amended 2024). **UNVERIFIED:** secondary sources only; the resolution text was not opened.

## 7. Why it matters for misinformation

1. **Disinformation is now a hired service.**
   - OII found organised social-media manipulation in 81 countries.
   - State actors worked with private firms in 48 of them.
   - Almost US$60 million has gone to such firms since 2009.
   - The supply of low-paid workers is part of that market (OII, 13 Jan 2021; URLs in 1.2).

2. **In the Philippines, the work hides in ordinary industries.**
   - Advertising and PR strategists design the campaigns and pass the work down to precarious workers paid by quota.
   - The workers come from the same pool as call centres and content moderation (Ong & Cabañes 2018; Washington Post 2019).
   - Platform takedowns show the reach. About 3.6 million accounts followed at least one Gabunada-network Page (Meta 2019). A military- and police-linked network mixed anti-communist content with posts about activists and the opposition (Meta 2020).

3. **Elections are the main target.**
   - **2025 midterm season:**
     - Cyabra found fake profiles behind about 37% of 2,154 X profiles discussing the elections, and up to 45% in a second sample.
     - The Star / Reuters, 15 Apr 2025: https://thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2025/04/15/duterte-praise-philippine-election-spin-driven-by-fake-accounts-study
   - **A domestic marketing firm** mass-produced partisan comments with AI (OpenAI, June 2025).
   - **Freedom House** reported that content manipulation surged during the Marcos–Duterte feud. It rated the Philippines 61/100, "Partly Free" (coverage June 2024 to May 2025).
     - **URL:** https://freedomhouse.org/country/philippines/freedom-net/2025
   - **Team Jorge** claimed involvement in 33 presidential campaigns. This is its own unverifiable claim (Forbidden Stories, 15 Feb 2023).

4. **It harms public debate and the people who hold power to account.**
   - In Kenya, paid campaigns targeted judges, journalists and activists, and led some to self-censor (Mozilla, 2021).
   - Foreign actors buy cheap outsourced labour to work on another country's divisions. The Ghana and Nigeria network posted about US race and policing (Meta, 2020).
   - Foreign states can also hire through local PR firms. The 2025 Senate allegation concerned the Chinese Embassy (PTV, 2025).
   - AidData found Beijing's influencer and troll-farm efforts in the Philippines had limited reach so far.
     - **Publisher:** Philstar
     - **Date:** 11 Sep 2024
     - **URL:** https://philstar.com/movies/2024/09/11/2384510/filipino-influencers-troll-farms-tapped-pro-china-narratives-study

5. **The scam economy and influence operations share tools and labour.** The same platforms, fake-account methods and AI tools serve both.
   - Scam centres use large language models and translation software to reach victims in new languages (INTERPOL, 2023).
   - UNODC describes crime groups selling "services" in a franchise-like model (UNODC, 21 Jul 2026).
   - Cutting off the supply of unwitting workers weakens both industries.
